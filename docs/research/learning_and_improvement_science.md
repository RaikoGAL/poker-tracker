# Learning and Improvement Science for Poker (what actually makes players improve and become profitable)

Scope: online cash NLHE, 6–9 handed, recreational-to-semi-pro. Notes are intended to let a PM define "learning from each session" concretely. Where a claim is practitioner opinion rather than data, it is flagged as **[folk/practitioner]**. Where it is from a peer-reviewed study or a simulation with stated assumptions, it is flagged **[data]**.

## KQ1. Highest-leverage study activity and study-to-play ratio

### Takeaway
Practitioner consensus (not controlled evidence) is that the highest-leverage study for a recreational/semi-pro cash player is structured review of *their own* hands — biggest losing pots plus hands they felt unsure about — combined with database/HUD self-review to find leaks, with solvers used to understand *why* a line works rather than to memorize. Recommended study:play ratios vary with level: roughly 50:50 for novices down to ~20:80 for advanced players; a "1 hour study per 1 hour play" rule is widely repeated for beginners. No academic study measures study-ratio effects on win rate.

### Cited Findings
- BlackRain79 (micro-stakes pro/author) recommends: beginners ~80% study / 20% play for the first weeks; novices "50% of their time studying at most"; advanced players "should only study 20% of the time" because "you don't get paid to study the game." For novices and advanced players, "self study" via HUD/database (PokerTracker/HM) is the core activity: review sessions regularly, run database filters to check profitability of specific plays, find/fix leaks, study opponents. **[practitioner]** — [BlackRain79](https://www.blackrain79.com/2016/11/study-versus-play-poker.html?m=1)
- The "1:1 study:play ratio" rule circulating in poker media is attributed to BlackRain79's own experience, not to research or other coaches; prioritized activities are consistent routine, hand review in PokerTracker, session reviews to find leaks, and studying winning players' strategies. **[practitioner]** — [PokerSkill](https://pokerskill.com/c/strategy-guides/the-crucial-11-study-play-ratio-rule-every-poker-player-should-know)
- A weekly one-hour review routine proposed by a training-site article: 10 min scan results and note mood/discipline; 20 min review and label five biggest losing hands; 20 min study 3–4 "unsure" hands with solver/training site/study partner; 10 min write one specific leak and one drill for the coming week (e.g., "fold more often to river check-raises with one pair"). Weekly hour ≈ 50 focused study hours/year. Emphasizes depth over volume. **[practitioner]** — [DeucesCracked hand review routine](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-hand-history-review-routine-find-leaks-2026)
- Solver guidance from the same source: "Use solvers to understand why a line works, not to memorize it"; treat solver output as a balanced baseline then consider exploitative deviations vs. real opponents; a study partner near your level is recommended for second opinions. **[practitioner]** — [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-hand-history-review-routine-find-leaks-2026)
- Dr. Tricia Cardner (poker psychologist, PokerNews) applying Ericsson's deliberate practice to poker: (1) focus narrowly — "You must have very specific and concrete goals for each study session"; (2) study beyond current skill (discomfort, not flow); (3) get immediate, accurate feedback — poker is hard here because review is delayed and results reward wrong decisions; (4) "repeat a skill so many times that it becomes unconsciously automatic." Also: elite performers limit intense deliberate practice to ~2–3 hours/day. **[practitioner citing research]** — [PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/deliberate-practice-four-steps-to-improve-your-poker-game-20411.htm)
- Tendler's "Inchworm": skill is a bell curve; improvement comes "from two sides: improving weakness and improving upon your best." The most efficient next step after reaching a new peak is to fix the worst (back-end) part of your range; eliminating C-game "frees mental space to learn new things." Judge progress by comparing "your worst to your previous worst," not to your recent peak. **[practitioner]** — [Hendon Mob excerpt of The Mental Game of Poker](https://www.thehendonmob.com/?p=1929)
- Tendler's Adult Learning Model: skills pass through unconscious incompetence → conscious incompetence → conscious competence → unconscious competence; learning that skips stages "is imaginary"; flaws are skills that became automatic and that you no longer want. Prioritize moving the bottom of the range up. **[practitioner]** — [TMGOP reader notes](https://alexspuffstuff.blogspot.com/2016/07/tmgop-notes.html?m=1)

### Inferences
- For the target user (recreational/semi-pro), the product's "learning loop" should default to a small number of *self-selected or auto-flagged hands* (5–15 per session/week) rather than mass stat dumps; every practitioner source converged on "few hands, deep, labeled, outcome-independent."
- A "study budget" feature could nudge toward the practitioner ratio: novices ~1:1 study:play; semi-pros ~1:4, with study time logged against play time.
- The weakest link in poker's deliberate-practice fit is feedback latency and noise (Cardner). A product that shortens the gap between decision and *process* feedback (e.g., same-day review prompts, solver/EV comparison on flagged spots) directly addresses the acknowledged gap.

### Gaps
- No controlled or observational study was found that measures the effect of study ratio or study type (hand review vs. solver vs. mental game) on subsequent win rate. All ratio advice is practitioner opinion.
- Could not retrieve primary Upswing Poker / Run It Once / GTO Wizard blog articles on study routines (search returned mostly aggregators); the Mobius Poker "Stat Check Sunday" coaching video transcript did not load.

## KQ2. What a well-structured hand review contains; frameworks

### Takeaway
Across Duke ("resulting"), Tendler (process model, A/C-game, mental hand history), Angelo (quitting as a skill) and training-site routines, a good review (a) is done soon after the session, (b) isolates the *decision point* where the error began, (c) reconstructs what was known at the time (ranges, goal of the action, sizing fit, range-consistency), (d) labels the hand outcome-independently (mistake / cooler / unclear; technical vs. mental error), (e) tallies categories over time, and (f) ends with one concrete process goal. Duke's core rule: never evaluate or ask about a decision by leading with its outcome.

### Cited Findings
- Annie Duke defines "resulting" as "too tight a relationship between the quality of the outcome and the quality of the decision"; "You can't use outcome quality as a perfect signal of decision quality, not with a small sample size anyway." Chess vs. poker: in poker "I may have played the hand literally perfectly and still lost." Practical rules: "focus on process instead"; "don't be so hard on yourself when things go badly and don't be so proud of yourself when they go well"; when getting feedback, describe the hand with all relevant facts but *withhold the decision you made and the outcome* so the listener isn't anchored. **[practitioner]** — [Nautilus interview with Duke](https://nautil.us/the-resulting-fallacy-is-ruining-your-decisions-236901)
- Post-session checklist from a training-site guide: review within 24 hours "while your reasoning is still recoverable"; start with flagged hands, then largest losing *and* winning pots; for each hand answer the same five questions: (1) What was my read on the opponent's range on each street? (2) What was my goal with this bet/call/fold? (3) Did my sizing match that goal? (4) Which hands in my range would I play the same way? (5) Did the result influence my judgment? Limit to ~10–15 well-chosen hands per session; categorize each mistake (preflop, river call, river bluff, tilt, sizing) and add a tally mark; tag errors as *technical* (knowledge gap) vs. *mental* (emotional state); "If leaks cluster late in sessions or after bad beats, the fix is emotional management, not more study." After 2–3 weeks compare tallies to see which leaks shrink. **[practitioner]** — [DeucesCracked session review process](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-session-review-process-guide-2026)
- What to capture during play (same source): flag hands where unsure whether to call/fold/raise; large pots; unexpected opponent actions; hands where you felt frustrated, bored or overconfident. "Flagging takes seconds." — [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-session-review-process-guide-2026)
- Hand-selection and labeling (second training-site routine): sort by largest losing pots; review "unsure" hands ("the best learning material"); review showdown surprises to check range reads; occasionally review large winning pots since they "can hide thin value bets or bluffs that worked for the wrong reasons"; use consistent tags ("river-call," "bluff-fail," "unsure"); identify "the street where the error began, not just where the chips went in"; check position and stack depth; label each hand mistake / cooler / unclear; note mental state (tired, tilted, distracted, focused). **[practitioner]** — [DeucesCracked hand review routine](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-hand-history-review-routine-find-leaks-2026)
- Tendler's Process Model: Preparation (list problems, fix one at a time, ordered by size/cost/frequency/emotional impact) → Warm-up/Performance → Results/Evaluation (rate results, play, mentality and enjoyment 1–10; note what was executed especially well or badly) → Analysis (identify problems/solutions, look for hidden factors). Rule: remove crossover — don't analyze while playing. Session accountability: set a goal before sitting down; afterwards rate gameplay and mindset 1–10 and review discipline. Use measurable, short-term, process-oriented goals and limit how many at once. Base confidence on practiced process, not recent results. **[practitioner]** — [TMGOP notes](https://alexspuffstuff.blogspot.com/2016/07/tmgop-notes.html?m=1)
- Tendler's out-of-game "three-column" CBT exercise: write unfiltered thoughts/feelings, identify the distortions, write a rational retort addressing the real issue. In-game "injecting logic": recognize at set intervals, return to breath, ask "What is the gamestate?", use the rational retort. **[practitioner]** — [TMGOP notes](https://alexspuffstuff.blogspot.com/2016/07/tmgop-notes.html?m=1)
- Tommy Angelo (Elements of Poker): "I think of quitting as a skill set unto itself"; "every session of cash-game poker I ever play will end on a quit." Two situational rules: when winning, quit once the extra enjoyment from more winnings is smaller than the pain of giving back what you've won; when stuck and not enjoying yourself, it is acceptable to quit simply because you aren't having fun. Quit before decision-making slips (fatigue, boredom, irritation, bad run). No numeric stop-loss or time cap given. **[practitioner]** — [PokerNews on Angelo](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/it-s-okay-to-be-a-quitter-in-poker-in-fact-it-s-essential-19456.htm)
- Evidence from an adjacent judgment domain (UK intelligence analysts, N=50, randomized): a structured reasoning method (Analysis of Competing Hypotheses) "failed to improve accuracy" (MAE 0.26 vs 0.21 control, n.s.), whereas post-hoc statistical recalibration ("coherentization") cut error 35% and averaging ~3 analysts' judgments captured most of a 61% reduction. The study did not test outcome vs. process feedback or feedback timing. **[data; adjacent domain]** — [Mandel, Karvetski & Dhami 2018, JDM](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/1530E9DAE8F42B2E8FC2B5FE374DB50F/S1930297500006628a.pdf/boosting-intelligence-analysts-judgment-accuracy-what-works-what-fails.pdf)

### Inferences
- A product hand-review template that operationalizes the sources: **decision point** (street where error began) → **what I knew** (ranges, position, stack depth, reads) → **goal of action and sizing fit** → **alternatives considered** → **outcome-independent grade** (mistake / cooler / unclear) and **error type** (technical vs. mental) → **confidence** (not explicitly in sources; see Gaps) → **mental-state tag**. Showing the outcome *after* the self-grade, not before, directly implements Duke's anti-anchoring rule.
- The analyst-training finding is a caution: a heavyweight structured checklist is not automatically accuracy-improving; the lightweight tally-of-categories approach plus a second opinion (study partner / coach / solver) is more consistent with what helped in that study (aggregation of independent judgments).
- Tendler's and the training-site routines both independently land on *1–10 ratings of play and mindset per session* plus *one process goal per session* — a cheap, high-adoption data model.

### Gaps
- No source gave a validated scheme for recording *confidence* at the decision point in poker; the "confidence" element in the brief is supported only indirectly (Duke's calibration framing). Treat as a design hypothesis.
- Could not access Tendler's "mental hand history" 5-step template or his A-to-C game worksheet text directly (excerpts/notes did not include them); the concepts are confirmed but the exact steps are not cited here.
- No direct evidence found that decision journaling improves poker win rates; the general decision-journaling literature search returned mostly blog/aggregator content.

## KQ3. Which stats are meaningful at 50 / 200 / 1,000 / 10,000 hands; variance math; what to measure instead

### Takeaway
bb/100 is almost pure noise below ~10k hands and is still ±5–6 bb/100 at 100k hands (SD≈90 bb/100 online 6-max): a 5 bb/100 winner has only a ~56% chance of a winning 1,000-hand sample. Preflop frequency stats (VPIP, PFR, RFI, 3-bet, steal) converge fastest because they generate a data point nearly every orbit; postflop stats must be judged by *opportunities*, not hands. Academic data show skill is real and persistent but even top-vs-bottom-percentile groups need ~1.5k–7k hands before the better group leads 75% of the time. Practitioners therefore recommend checking results only every 10k–50k hands and tracking process metrics (tallied mistake categories, goal adherence, mental-state ratings) in between.

### Cited Findings
**Variance math**
- Online 6-max NL cash SD ≈ 90 bb/100 (range 80–100). With a 5 bb/100 true win rate, "noise is about 18 times the signal." 95% CI half-widths at SD=90: ±18 bb/100 at 10k hands; ±5.6 at 100k; ±2.5 at 500k; ±1.8 at 1M. Formula (derived, reproduces table): SE = SD/√(n/100); CI = ±1.96·SE; hands for half-width h: n = 100·(1.96·SD/h)². Implication: a 5 bb/100 winner with 100k hands has a 95% CI of roughly 0 to 10.6, so breakeven cannot be ruled out. Ordinary downswings for a winning cash player: 15–25 buy-ins. **[simulation/math]** — [DeucesCracked variance explainer](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-variance-explained-sample-size-win-rate)
- Mobius Poker coaching (uses SD 115 bb/100 in examples): a 5 bb/100 winner has a 44% chance of losing over 1,000 hands; results of ±5 buy-ins occur ~20% of the time; a 5 bb/100 winner has a 56% chance of a winning 1k sample vs 58% for a 7.5 bb/100 winner — i.e., a 50% win-rate improvement is nearly invisible in session results. Recommends a predetermined "Results Day" interval: not lower than 10k hands online, typically 10–50k (example: every 30k hands ≈ every 4 weeks at 7.5k hands/week). If within ~2 SD of EV, keep playing; a ≥2 SD downswing warrants time off and a reset. If you check too often, hide stats in tracker or hide the cashier. "The next 30,000-hand sample has nothing to do with the previous one." Doubting your edge is self-fulfilling: "you will actually play worse." **[practitioner + math]** — [Mobius: When and How to Check Results](https://mobiuspokercoaching.squarespace.com/blog/when-and-how-to-check-results)
- Breakeven stretches (1,000 simulated players, SD 115 then 90 bb/100): a 2.5 bb/100 winner has a 97% chance of at least one 100k-hand unprofitable stretch within 1M hands; a 7.5 bb/100 winner's expected longest unprofitable stretch is ~86k hands. Author deliberately uses a higher SD than his database shows because "real players tend to play worse during downswings." **[simulation]** — [Mobius: Beyond Downswings](https://mobiuspokercoaching.squarespace.com/blog/beyond-downswings-why-extended-breakeven-runs-are-inevitable)

**Academic evidence on skill persistence and hands needed**
- Potter van Loon, van den Assem & van Dolder (2015, PLoS ONE): 456M player-hand observations, 76.9M hands, 611k accounts, NLHE cash at $0.25/$2/$10 BB. Top-decile players (first 6 months) earned ~19.5 bb/100 more than bottom-decile in the next 6 months (p=0.002). Individual rank correlations across periods were modest: 0.074–0.104 (raw win rate), 0.091–0.148 for their Performance Robustness Measure (a t-statistic-like win rate adjusted for sample size/variance), which they argue ranks players better than raw win rate. Top-decile repeat rate 13.6% (raw) / 20.7% (PRM) vs 10% chance. Simulations: the more skilled group (top vs bottom percentile by PRM) wins >75% of the time after ~1,471 hands; by raw win rate 2,139 hands; by decile 6,512 (PRM) / 7,293 (raw). With a 21.2 bb/100 skill gap, the better group was *behind* 47% of the time after 10 hands, 29% after 1,000 hands, 4% after 10,000 hands. Rake averaged ~16 bb/100; only 32% of players profitable after rake. Playing more hands, tightness/aggressiveness (style), and tournament rating predicted future performance. **[data]** — [PMC4346402](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4346402)
- Levitt & Miles (NBER WP 17023, 2010 WSOP, 57 events, 32,496 players): a priori "high skill" players (720, identified from pre-2010 rankings) had ROI +30.5% vs −15.6% for everyone else (9.8% vs −13.8% excluding the Main Event); in pairwise matchups the skilled player wins 54.9%, comparable to MLB playoff teams vs non-playoff teams (55.7%). "Strong evidence in support of the idea that poker is a game of skill." **[data]** — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17023/w17023.pdf)

**Stat convergence**
- Ryan Laplante (pro) uses VPIP, PFR, RFI, 3-bet, steal on the HUD because they "converge into a reliable sample size over the shortest period of time" (a new data point almost every orbit). C-bet example: 300 hands yielded only 11 c-bet opportunities — "not a big enough sample size." Count *opportunities*, not hands, for postflop stats; turn/river stats are less reliable still; c-bet % ignores position, stack size, texture, and number of players. No hand-count thresholds given. **[practitioner]** — [PokerNews / Laplante](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/teachable-moments-from-twitch-ryan-laplante-hud-stats-32348.htm)
- Training-site guidance: review stats over several thousand hands; rare spots (e.g., river check-raises) need much larger samples; start with the stats that deviate most from reasonable ranges for the format and ask why. **[practitioner]** — [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-hand-history-review-routine-find-leaks-2026)

### Inferences
- A defensible "what to show at each sample size" ladder, derived from the above (the bands are my synthesis, not from a single source):
  - **≤200 hands (a session):** show *no* win-rate judgment. Show process data only: flagged hands, mistake tallies by category, mental-state ratings, goal adherence, session length, stop-loss adherence. Show VPIP/PFR only as "indicative" (an orbit-level stat accrues ~20–30 data points per 200 hands, enough to notice gross drift like VPIP 45% vs plan 24%).
  - **~1,000 hands:** preflop frequency stats by position become usable for *self-consistency* (is my UTG VPIP stable week to week?); 3-bet and fold-to-3-bet still thin; win rate meaningless (56% vs 44% coin flip for a 5 bb/100 winner). Skilled vs unskilled group difference is detectable only in the extreme-tail case (~1.5k hands at a 21 bb/100 gap).
  - **~10,000 hands:** earliest "Results Day" that practitioners endorse; 95% CI still ±18 bb/100; postflop frequency stats (c-bet, fold to c-bet) begin to be usable by opportunity count; a 20 bb/100 skill gap is now visible 96% of the time.
  - **50k–100k hands:** win rate ±5–8 bb/100; trends in red line/showdown winnings start to mean something; downswings of 15–25 buy-ins remain normal.
- The product should therefore be built around **Performance-Robustness-style displays** (win rate with confidence band and "hands until this band is ±X") rather than a bare bb/100, plus **outcome-independent process metrics**: (1) count/rate of hands self-labeled "mistake" per 100 hands, split technical vs mental; (2) share of marked mistakes occurring late-session or within N hands after a big loss (tilt indicator from the review guides); (3) preflop stat stability by position across sessions; (4) 1–10 self-ratings of play and mindset; (5) adherence to pre-committed session rules (time cap, stop-loss, goals).
- The Potter van Loon finding that *playing more hands* predicts future win rate is consistent with volume-driven learning, but the authors flag selection (better players choose to play more) — don't present volume as a lever in itself.

### Gaps
- No source gave empirical convergence thresholds (hands or opportunities) for specific HUD stats; the practitioner rule is "count opportunities," without numbers. A product could compute per-stat standard errors from opportunity counts (binomial SE) rather than relying on a published rule of thumb.
- No published data on whether *self-labeled mistake frequency* predicts later win rate; this is the central product hypothesis and is untested in the literature I found.

## KQ4. Tilt triggers and countermeasures; mood logging; sports-psychology evidence

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed poker-psychology work defines tilt as a frustration-induced loss of behavioral/emotional/cognitive control, finds sensitivity to losses (especially perceived *injustice* from bad beats) is the strongest predictor of tilt severity, and shows experience alone does not reduce tilt — only experience that the player has consciously converted into coping does. Problem-gambling-risk players report tilting ~70% more often than low-risk players. No experimental test of stop-loss or session caps exists in the poker literature; practitioner countermeasures (pre-commitment, time caps, interval emotion checks, cooldowns) are supported indirectly by a sports meta-analysis showing pre-performance routines improve performance for novices and elites alike, and by responsible-gambling data showing voluntary limit-setting reduces spend among the most intense players.

### Cited Findings
- Moreau et al. (2020, online poker tilt scale, N=291 French-speaking players, 93% men): tilt is "a sudden and uncontrolled change in gambling behavior" induced by frustration, affecting behavioral, emotional and cognitive processes; "tilt happens to all players, novices or experienced, in control or at risk." Core triggers named: frustration from "bad luck" and "other players' behaviour," losing, and the irrational belief that lost money can be recovered. Self-reported tilt frequency means: 3.8 (problem gamblers), 3.5 (moderate risk), 2.2 (low risk). Tilt scale total correlated r≈.54–.56 with the Problem Gambling Severity Index; the authors call tilt possibly "a gateway to this disorder." Players reportedly view *identifying tilt and stopping play* as the only adequate response. **No intervention (stop-loss, breaks, limits) was tested.** **[data]** — [Moreau et al. PDF](https://cdspress.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Moreau-et-al..pdf)
- Palomäki, Laakasuo & Salmela (2013, "Losing more by losing it," N=417 Finnish players): sensitivity to losses (feelings of injustice, anger, frustration after losses) was the strongest predictor of tilt severity (B=0.598, p<.001). Contrary to expectation, more experience was associated with *more* severe tilting (B=0.22) unless the player perceived that experience had taught them to tilt less (PEET): among experienced players with high PEET, tilt severity was much lower (B=−0.48). Experience reduced loss sensitivity only marginally in the full model (p=.08). Triggers listed: bad beats "losing when winning seemed very likely," losing streaks, fatigue, being needled. Cross-sectional self-report; causal claims cautioned. **[data]** — [Palomäki dissertation incl. Study III](https://core.ac.uk/download/17340392.pdf)
- Tendler's tilt taxonomy and in-game protocol: tilt types include entitlement, revenge, desperation, anger, boredom (the reader notes omit running-bad/injustice/hate-losing/mistake tilt which appear in the book); "tilt hierarchy" — under tilt higher functions shut down leaving unconscious habits in control; "Injecting logic" protocol: check emotional state at set intervals (e.g., every song change), return to breath, ask "what is the gamestate?", apply a prepared rational retort; recurring issues → remind yourself of the already-known solution. Warm-up before playing "so you're playing fully and well from the start"; post-session 1–10 ratings of play, mentality, enjoyment. **[practitioner]** — [TMGOP notes](https://alexspuffstuff.blogspot.com/2016/07/tmgop-notes.html?m=1)
- Training-site review guide: log hands where you felt frustrated, bored or overconfident; tag mental vs technical errors; if leaks cluster late in sessions or after bad beats, "the fix is emotional management, not more study"; keep a tilt tally category. **[practitioner]** — [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-session-review-process-guide-2026)
- Session-length vendor guide (Poker Analytics): no stop-loss thresholds; recommends setting a time limit and alarm before sitting down ("a reminder rather than a law"), leaving when the game toughens or after a big loss ("the next twenty minutes after a big loss won't be your best poker" — no data); illustrative arithmetic: at 5 bb/100 and 25 hands/hr, "one 5 BB mistake at the end of a session costs you four hours of play." No research cited; claims are opinion. **[folk/vendor]** — [Poker Analytics](https://www.poker-analytics.net/guides/how-long-should-a-poker-session-be.html)
- Angelo's quitting rules (see KQ2): quit before decision quality slips; no numeric stop-loss. **[practitioner]** — [PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/it-s-okay-to-be-a-quitter-in-poker-in-fact-it-s-essential-19456.htm)
- Sports psychology: meta-analysis by Rupprecht, Tran & Gröpel (Univ. of Vienna, Int. Rev. Sport & Exercise Psych. 2021), ~800 athletes across 15 sports: performance significantly improved after learning and applying a pre-performance routine; effect held under pressure and in real competition; "The results support performance benefits of a routine for novices and elite athletes alike." Effect size not reported in the release. **[data; adjacent domain]** — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/934597)
- Responsible-gambling data (Kindred operator, 49,560 players, 1-year follow-up): among the most gambling-intense players (top 10% by amount wagered), those who voluntarily set deposit/spend limits spent significantly less a year later; no effect across the whole sample; no effect sizes in the abstract; self-selection not ruled out. **[data; adjacent]** — [Auer & Griffiths, NTU repository](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/38900)
- Mobius: a ≥2 SD downswing on a Results Day should trigger time off, talking to someone and a reset; expectation-setting (knowing breakeven runs are normal) is the recommended protection against abandoning strategy. **[practitioner]** — [Mobius](https://mobiuspokercoaching.squarespace.com/blog/when-and-how-to-check-results)

### Inferences
- The Palomäki result is the single most product-relevant finding: experience does not fix tilt by itself; *reflecting on and recognizing one's own tilt patterns* is what moderates it. That is a direct argument for structured mood/tilt logging with feedback (a "tilt profile" that shows the player their own triggers and late-session error clustering), not just a mood emoji.
- Because perceived *injustice* after losses is the dominant driver, a cooldown prompt after large lost pots (which the player's hand history makes detectable automatically) is better targeted than a generic time-based break.
- Stop-loss/time-cap rules are untested in poker research but are consistent with (a) players' own view that stopping is the only adequate response to tilt, (b) voluntary limit-setting reducing spend in the heaviest users, and (c) pre-commitment routines helping athletes regardless of skill. Treat them as well-supported-by-analogy, not proven.
- Mood logging before/after: no direct poker data. Tendler's 1–10 mindset rating and the training-site "note mood and discipline" step are the practitioner standard; the Palomäki mechanism suggests the value is in the *feedback* (seeing patterns), so logging without a review surface is likely low-value.

### Gaps
- Could not access the Moreau/Sévigny "Tilt in Online Poker: Loss of Control and Gambling Disorder" PMC article (reCAPTCHA); the related OPTS-9 paper was used instead.
- No study found that measures whether stop-loss rules, session caps, or cooldown timers change poker results or tilt frequency. No data found on session length vs. win rate (only vendor claims).
- No poker-specific or general evidence found that *pre-session mood logging* itself improves performance; evidence is indirect (routines, self-recognition of tilt).

## KQ5. Bankroll management: recommendations, risk-of-ruin math, move-down rules

### Takeaway
Standard online 6-max cash guidance is 30–50 buy-ins (20–25 only with a documented win rate >4 bb/100 over 10k+ hands); risk of ruin follows RoR = e^(−2·B·μ/σ²), so a 3 bb/100 winner at SD 90 needs ~62 buy-ins for 1% lifetime ruin, and a 2 bb/100 winner with 20 buy-ins busts >25% of the time within 100k hands. Common move-down triggers are <20 buy-ins or a 15-buy-in downswing; shot-taking caps risk at ~4 buy-ins at the new stake. The evidence that these rules reduce busting is simulation-based (deterministic math), plus indirect responsible-gambling data on voluntary limits; no field study of poker move-down rules exists.

### Cited Findings
- Closed-form lifetime risk of ruin: RoR = e^(−2Bμ/σ²) (B bankroll, μ win rate, σ SD, all in bb/100). 100k-hand bust probabilities (40,000 simulated careers, σ=90): 1 bb/100 winner — 61.3% with 10 buy-ins, 35.4% with 20, 9.1% with 40; 2 bb/100 — 25.6% with 20, 4.7% with 40; 3 bb/100 — 17.4% with 20, 2.4% with 40, 0.8% with 50; 5 bb/100 — 7.3% with 20, 0.5% with 40; 8 bb/100 — 1.7% with 20. Lifetime buy-ins for 5%/1%/0.1% ruin: 1 bb/100 → 121/187/280; 2 → 61/93/140; 3 → 40/62/93; 4 → 30/47/70; 5 → 24/37/56; 8 → 15/23/35. SD effect at 3 bb/100 and 40 buy-ins: σ60 <0.1%, σ90 2.5%, σ110 7.2%, σ140 (PLO) 16.7% — risk scales with σ². Tilt example: lifetime ruin for 40 buy-ins rises from 1.9% at 4 bb/100 to 37.2% at 1 bb/100. Recommends 40–50 buy-ins for solid mid-stakes regulars; 20 is "dangerous unless you are winning well above 5 bb/100"; beyond ~50 extra bankroll is better used for stake mobility. **[simulation/math]** — [DeucesCracked RoR study](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-risk-of-ruin-variance-simulation-study)
- Rule set ("traffic light"): Green 50+ buy-ins; Yellow 25–30 = "study mode" (reassess win rate, tighten game selection, no shots); Red <20 = drop stakes immediately. Move up at 50 buy-ins at current stake while keeping 40 at the lower stake. Move down below 20 buy-ins or after a 15-buy-in downswing or after a failed shot. Shot-taking: only at 50+ buy-ins, risk max 4 buy-ins at the new stake, retreat if lost. Online needs more than live (e.g., 40+ online vs 25–30 live at equivalent stakes). A 2–3 bb/100 winner at σ 90–100 faces a 20+ buy-in downswing roughly once per 100k hands. Rake can cost 3–4 bb/100 when moving to a higher cap. "Earned" lower requirement: 10k+ hands at 2+ bb/100 supports 20–25 buy-ins. Track bb/100, hands per session and downswing depth monthly. **[practitioner + math]** — [DeucesCracked bankroll rules](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/cash-game-bankroll-management-2026-rules-traffic-light-system)
- Generic bankroll guidance: full-ring cash 20–30 buy-ins; 6-max 30–50; HU/short-stack 50–100; large-field MTT 200–300 minimum. Ordinary downswing for a winning cash player 15–25 buy-ins. **[practitioner]** — [DeucesCracked variance explainer](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-variance-explained-sample-size-win-rate)
- Mixing stakes can *extend* a downswing because you may need to drop down to recover losses. **[practitioner/simulation commentary]** — [Mobius](https://mobiuspokercoaching.squarespace.com/blog/beyond-downswings-why-extended-breakeven-runs-are-inevitable)
- Voluntary limit-setting reduced spend over a year among the top-10% most intense gamblers (N=49,560, one operator); not across all players; self-selection possible. **[data; adjacent]** — [Auer & Griffiths](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/38900)
- Potter van Loon et al.: rake averaged ~16 bb/100 at the stakes studied; only 32% of players were net profitable after rake vs 37.5% before. **[data]** — [PMC4346402](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4346402)

### Inferences
- Because true win rate is unknowable at recreational sample sizes (KQ3), bankroll rules should be driven by a *conservative lower-confidence-bound* win rate, not the point estimate: e.g., a player showing 5 bb/100 over 20k hands has a 95% lower bound near −8 bb/100 and should be rolled as if a ~1–2 bb/100 winner (≈60–90 buy-ins for 1–5% lifetime ruin), or accept a higher ruin probability knowingly.
- The RoR formula makes the "tilt tax" concrete: the same 40-buy-in roll goes from ~2% to ~37% lifetime ruin if tilt cuts win rate from 4 to 1 bb/100 — a quantitative bridge between the mental-game features and the bankroll features.
- Move-down and shot rules are pre-commitment devices; the responsible-gambling finding that voluntary limits help only the most intense users suggests they matter most for the heaviest-volume/most-at-risk players in the app.

### Gaps
- No field study found that compares busting rates between players who follow move-down rules and those who do not; all evidence is simulation (which is exact under its assumptions) or analogical.
- The 15-buy-in-downswing and 4-buy-in-shot thresholds are practitioner conventions; no source derived them from data.

## KQ6. Deliberate practice, feedback timing, spaced repetition; self-explanation/journaling evidence

### Takeaway
The deliberate-practice framework (Ericsson) maps cleanly onto poker in practitioner writing — narrow goals, work beyond comfort, immediate accurate feedback, repetition to automaticity, limited daily intensity — but poker violates the "immediate accurate feedback" condition, which is exactly where review tooling adds value. I found no poker-specific experimental evidence for spaced repetition or self-explanation, and the one rigorous adjacent experiment (structured reasoning method for analysts) found the method did *not* improve accuracy, cautioning against assuming any journaling template works.

### Cited Findings
- Cardner/Ericsson applied to poker: quality over quantity; specific concrete goals each study session; study beyond current skill; immediate accurate feedback is "hard" in poker because "hand reviews happen after sessions, and results can reward wrong decisions while punishing right ones"; repeat until automatic; elite performers practice deliberately only ~2–3 hours/day. **[practitioner citing Ericsson]** — [PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/deliberate-practice-four-steps-to-improve-your-poker-game-20411.htm)
- Tendler's four-stage learning model (unconscious incompetence → unconscious competence) and the requirement to train a correction "until it is trained" (reach unconscious competence) before moving on; repetition and single-focus are the stated mechanism. **[practitioner]** — [Hendon Mob excerpt](https://www.thehendonmob.com/?p=1929); [TMGOP notes](https://alexspuffstuff.blogspot.com/2016/07/tmgop-notes.html?m=1)
- Weekly "one leak + one drill" loop and 2–3-week tally comparison as the practitioner implementation of focused repetition with delayed feedback. **[practitioner]** — [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-hand-history-review-routine-find-leaks-2026); [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-session-review-process-guide-2026)
- Randomized test of a structured analytic reasoning method with professional analysts: no accuracy gain (and a non-significant trend toward *worse*), while statistical recalibration and aggregating ~3 independent judgments did help. **[data; adjacent]** — [Mandel et al. 2018](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/1530E9DAE8F42B2E8FC2B5FE374DB50F/S1930297500006628a.pdf/boosting-intelligence-analysts-judgment-accuracy-what-works-what-fails.pdf)
- Pre-performance routines improve performance for novices and elites alike (~800 athletes, 15 sports). **[data; adjacent]** — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/934597)

### Inferences
- The highest-value product intervention under the deliberate-practice lens is **shortening and de-noising feedback**: (a) review within 24h; (b) compare the chosen line to a reference (solver/EV/coach) rather than to the pot result; (c) show the player their own repeat-error categories so repetition is targeted. This is what "immediate, accurate feedback" can realistically mean in poker.
- Spaced repetition has a natural fit for *preflop ranges by position* and *recurring leak categories* (re-surface a flagged spot type a few days later as a quiz), but this is an inference from general learning science, not from poker evidence.
- Given the analyst-study result, the product should A/B test review templates against a minimal "tag + tally" baseline rather than assume more structure is better.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed studies found on spaced repetition, self-explanation, or decision journaling *in poker*; the general decision-journal search returned only blogs/aggregators, so I could not cite primary evidence for journaling improving decision quality.
- Ericsson's primary papers were not fetched (search surfaced secondary sources); the deliberate-practice principles above are as relayed by Cardner.

## KQ7. What a plateau looks like in data and what breaks it

### Takeaway
Direct literature on poker plateaus is essentially absent. The best available framing combines (a) variance data showing that flat 50k–100k-hand stretches are statistically *expected* even for 2.5–7.5 bb/100 winners, so a "plateau" cannot be declared from results alone at recreational volumes, and (b) Tendler's Inchworm, which predicts plateaus when the player only works on the front of their range (peak play) while the back end (C-game) stays fixed. Practitioners' plateau-breakers: switch focus to the worst-executed spots, mix stakes/sites, get a second opinion (coach/partner), and re-derive one specific leak per week.

### Cited Findings
- 97% probability of a ≥100k-hand unprofitable stretch within 1M hands for a 2.5 bb/100 winner; expected longest unprofitable stretch ~86k hands at 7.5 bb/100 (SD 115, with a 90-SD addendum). "Don't assume you've 'forgotten' how to play"; avoid abandoning strategy during the stretch. **[simulation]** — [Mobius](https://mobiuspokercoaching.squarespace.com/blog/beyond-downswings-why-extended-breakeven-runs-are-inevitable)
- Mobius cannot give a firm rule for how long a breakeven run must last before changing the game plan: "the longer the losses or break-even stretch, the lower the odds it's 'just variance'"; suggests dropping stakes or changing sites temporarily to refresh mindset and diversify. **[practitioner]** — [Mobius](https://mobiuspokercoaching.squarespace.com/blog/when-and-how-to-check-results)
- Inchworm: "Improvement happens from two sides"; avoiding weaknesses "flattens the curve"; after a front-end step the efficient next move is the back-end step (fix the worst). Measure progress by worst-vs-previous-worst. **[practitioner]** — [Hendon Mob](https://www.thehendonmob.com/?p=1929); [TMGOP notes](https://alexspuffstuff.blogspot.com/2016/07/tmgop-notes.html?m=1)
- Deliberate practice: people "gravitate toward material that feels easy, but that won't produce improvement"; improvement requires leaving flow. **[practitioner citing Ericsson]** — [PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/deliberate-practice-four-steps-to-improve-your-poker-game-20411.htm)
- Potter van Loon et al.: skill differences are stable over time (Deming coefficients 0.86–1.11 at fixed hand counts), i.e., absent intervention, a player's relative standing tends to persist. **[data]** — [PMC4346402](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4346402)

### Inferences
- In product data, a *real* plateau is better defined on process metrics than on results: the self-labeled mistake rate per 100 hands stops falling, the same 1–2 error categories dominate the tally for 3+ review cycles, mental-state ratings don't improve, and late-session error clustering persists — while results-based "plateau" should be gated behind the Results-Day sample sizes from KQ3.
- Plateau interventions supported by the sources: (1) force a "back-end step" — pick the worst-executed category and make it the sole weekly drill; (2) introduce a second judge (study partner/coach/solver comparison) since independent judgments improved accuracy where structured self-method did not; (3) change environment (stake/site) to reset mindset; (4) reduce session length / add warm-up if mental errors dominate.

### Gaps
- No study was found that characterizes poker plateaus empirically or tests interventions that break them; the chess-coaching analogies surfaced in search were not fetched because they are blogs without data.
- I could not retrieve a primary GTO Wizard / Run It Once / Upswing / Pokercode source on plateau coaching; the Mobius "Stat Check Sunday" coaching review (which would show how a coach diagnoses a stuck NL100–200 player's stats) failed to load.

## Cross-cutting summary for the PM (synthesis; each element traces to the cited sections)

**Capture per session (cheap, ≤1 minute):** pre-session goal (one process goal) and intended time cap/stop-loss; in-session hand flags with a reason tag (unsure / big pot / showdown surprise / frustrated-bored-overconfident); post-session 1–10 ratings of play, mindset, enjoyment; discipline check (did I follow the cap/goal?). Sources: Tendler Process Model; DeucesCracked review guides.

**Review (within 24h, 5–15 hands):** decision point → what I knew → goal and sizing fit → alternatives → outcome-independent label (mistake/cooler/unclear) → technical vs mental → compare to a reference (solver/partner). Reveal outcome after grading. Sources: Duke; DeucesCracked; Tendler.

**Show the player:** mistake tallies by category and trend over 2–3-week windows; late-session / post-big-loss clustering (tilt indicator); preflop stat stability by position; bankroll health in buy-ins with the RoR implication at a conservative win-rate estimate; win rate *only* with a confidence band and a Results-Day cadence (≥10k hands). Sources: KQ3, KQ4, KQ5.

**Measure "getting better":** falling self-labeled mistake rate per 100 hands; shrinking share of mental errors; rising goal/limit adherence; stable preflop frequencies; and only at 50k+ hand horizons, win-rate band shifting upward. The Palomäki finding implies tilt improvement should be measured as *recognized-and-acted-on* tilt episodes, not just fewer bad moods.

**Flagged as folk wisdom (no data found):** specific study:play ratios; numeric stop-loss and time caps; 15-buy-in move-down and 4-buy-in shot thresholds; "20 minutes after a big loss is bad poker"; plateau-breaking recipes.
