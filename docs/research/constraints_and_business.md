# Constraints and Business Realities: Poker Self-Improvement App for Club-App Players (Israel-founded)

Research date: 2026-11-05. Each finding is tagged with the source's date where known. Findings are labelled as LAW (statute/case law), POLICY (platform or app-store rule), or PRACTICE (observed enforcement / market behaviour). Nothing here is legal advice.

## Key Question 1: What do ClubGG / PokerBros / PPPoker terms permit for third-party tools?

### Takeaway
None of the three club apps' official terms pages could be fetched directly (PokerBros and PPPoker returned errors, ClubGG blocks crawlers), so the policy picture below is assembled from secondary sources and must be verified against the live in-app T&Cs. Secondary sources consistently describe all three as banning external software (PokerBros: only its own HUD; PPPoker: "simulators and third-party software"), with weak or no documented enforcement against post-session tools; the October 2026 superuser scandal has sharply tightened the industry mood against *any* third-party software and introduced screenshot-blocking "screen guards" at GGPoker (ClubGG's parent NSUS), which is the single biggest emerging practical risk for a screenshot-based review tool.

### Cited Findings
- PPPoker (POLICY, reported 2021-03-22): PPPoker added a term that "Prohibits the usage of simulators and third-party software." The article notes the open question was hand converters used for HUDs, that there were "no precedents of sanctions against players for third-party software," and that a converter's status "may depend on individual club administrators." PPPoker's own built-in HUD is suggested as the fallback. Detection described only via an "Auto Interception System" tracking ~100 behaviour indicators for collusion/bots. — [WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/blog/software-ban-and-other-new-features-from-the-pppoker-security-team)
- PPPoker (POLICY paraphrase, 2026-05-16, promotional source): PPPoker bans software that "alters game outcomes, gives unauthorized assistance, or automates gameplay"; penalties "range from temporary suspensions to permanent bans"; PPPoker "often investigates before making punitive decisions." The article never names HUDs, RTA, data mining, screen scraping or equity calculators and quotes no policy text. ClubGG is said to ban "tools that affect game integrity"; PokerBros' policy "includes investigations into suspicious activity." Source is a vendor (pokerhack.org / Reveal Poker) blog; treat as low-reliability. — [dev.to / pokerhack.org](https://dev.to/pokerhackorg/analyzing-penalty-policies-for-third-party-software-on-pppoker-an8)
- PokerBros (POLICY per review, last updated 2026-07-25): "the terms allow only the PokerBros HUD and prohibit other external tools"; terms prohibit cheating and collusion and reference a "Game Integrity Bros" team. Review also states collusion/bot allegations are "widely reported, with no investigation or enforcement." Owner: ThinkLean Limited; launched 2019. — [PokerListings PokerBros review](https://www.pokerlistings.com/pokerbros-app-review)
- PRACTICE: Hand2Note (a commercial HUD/tracker) publicly lists PPPoker and PokerBros as supported Android apps run through a Windows emulator (LDPlayer recommended) with a HUD overlaid on the emulator window, and lists ClubGG under desktop sites. Hand2Note claims it is "officially allowed on all of the sites listed above," but the page gives no per-app permission evidence for the club apps. — [Hand2Note supported sites](https://hand2note4-com-test-dzf5dpdueud7cqdm.westeurope-01.azurewebsites.net/Help/supported-sites)
- PRACTICE (2026-05-28): An industry roundup of HUD compliance contains no policy statements at all for PPPoker, PokerBros, ClubGG or KKPoker, versus explicit lists for PokerStars (approved-tools list, RTA/datamining/shared databases prohibited), GGPoker (no HUDs), partypoker (stricter approved list) and WPT Global (no HUDs). — [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/best-poker-hud-software-2026-pokerstars-vs-ggpoker-compliance)
- GGPoker / NSUS (POLICY, 2026-10-18): GGPoker banned all third-party software (named: Jurojin Poker, Holdem Manager 3, PokerTracker 4), including hand-history collation tools, stating: "Previously, we permitted certain third-party tools that did not affect gameplay. However, following the recent incident, we are tightening this policy to better protect our players and reduce the risk of a similar incident." GGPoker also announced a screen guard that blocks screenshots and recordings of open tables, and win-rate anomaly detection for RTA. — [PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2026/10/ggpoker-bans-huds-52622.htm)
- Superuser scandal context (PRACTICE, 2026-09-30 to 2026-10-09): A tampered update of the third-party tool Jurojin Poker installed "Mesh" spyware that let an attacker see players' screens; one player believes he lost at least $100,000. Jurojin's remediation included removing periodic hand saving "so less play data is stored on users' PCs" and adding supply-chain security checks. — [PokerNews](https://pokernews.com/news/2026/10/jurojin-speaks-after-superuser-scandal-52575.htm)
- partypoker (POLICY, 2026-07-13, promotional article): GTO solvers/training software (PioSOLVER, GTO Wizard, Flopzilla, Equilab) prohibited "while the client is running"; Universal Replayer (a hand review tool) is on the approved list. Use between hands or post-session is not directly addressed. — [PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/news/promotions/2026/06/does-partypoker-allow-huds-in-2026-51434.htm)
- Historical precedent on data-mined databases (PRACTICE, undated, c. 2010): PokerStars' legal action forced Poker Table Ratings to remove player profiles; Poker-Edge pre-emptively removed its player search. Data mining is framed as prohibited by all major sites. — [Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/news/media-and-software/poker-edge-removes-all-public-profiles/)
- ClubGG iOS listing (POLICY/PRACTICE, fetched 2026-11-05): Developer NSUS Ltd; rated 18+ with "Simulated Gambling (frequent)" and "Contests"; sells Diamonds ($0.99-$99.99), Casino Chips, Standard ($9.99) and Platinum ($49.99) memberships; 954 ratings, 2.7/5; no statement on real money. — [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1529839330)

### Inferences
- All three club apps are run as "play-money" products whose terms treat real-money exchange as happening outside the app; their third-party-software clauses are written to protect that fiction and the operators' integrity teams, not to distinguish in-play vs post-session tools. A post-session review tool built from the player's own screenshots is not expressly addressed by any policy text located; it is neither clearly permitted nor clearly banned.
- The practical enforcement power sits with club owners/agents, not the app vendor (PPPoker explicitly: status "may depend on individual club administrators"). Distribution risk is therefore reputational at the club level more than a vendor ban.
- An equity calculator used between hands is in the same category as Equilab/Flopzilla, which partypoker bans "while the client is running"; on mobile (where the poker app is foregrounded) the distinction is academic, but no club app policy on this was found.
- The GGPoker screen guard (same corporate parent as ClubGG) is the most concrete threat: if NSUS ports screenshot blocking to ClubGG, a screenshot-ingestion workflow breaks for that app. No evidence found yet that ClubGG has done so.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the actual T&C text of pokerbros.net, clubgg.net or pppoker.net (fetch errors / robots block). The exact clauses on HUDs, screen recording, reverse engineering and hand-history sharing need to be read inside each app or via a browser.
- No documented 2024-2026 enforcement example (ban, confiscation) specifically for third-party software use on PPPoker, PokerBros or ClubGG was found; the only enforcement coverage concerns collusion/bots.

## Key Question 2: Legal position in Israel (poker, club apps, and companion tools)

### Takeaway
Under Israeli Penal Law Chapter 12, poker has been treated by criminal courts as a "prohibited game" (organising: up to 3 years / ILS 452,000; playing: up to 1 year / ILS 29,200), online gambling is prohibited except the state sports-betting monopoly, and B2B gambling licences do not exist; the Supreme Court's 2018 Amit (Amishvili Rafi) tax ruling suggested professional poker may be skill-based but only in obiter. A tool that handles no money and offers no gambling is not addressed by any located source; the ancillary-services restriction in the Penal Law is described in terms of venues, and no Israeli enforcement against software vendors to players was found.

### Cited Findings
- LAW: Penal Law Chapter 12 covers "prohibited game," lottery and betting with deliberately broad, overlapping definitions; a "predominance test" (skill vs chance) applies; s.225 criminalises organising/conducting (up to 3 years or ILS 452,000), s.226 criminalises playing a prohibited game (up to 1 year or ILS 29,200); restrictions also bar services ancillary to gambling "such as operating venues"; social gaming exemption under s.230 is read narrowly. — [Chambers Gaming Law 2025, Israel (Liran Barak, Herzog Fox & Neeman)](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/gaming-law-2025/israel/law-and-practice)
- LAW: "case law has classified poker as a prohibited game under Section 224." In Civil Appeal 476/17 Amit Amishvili Rafi v Assessing Officer Tel Aviv 4 (2018) the Supreme Court majority "suggested poker may not be a game of chance, especially for an experienced professional" — obiter, not binding; it criticised lower courts for assessing an amateur playing a single hand. — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/gaming-law-2025/israel/law-and-practice)
- LAW: Online gambling is prohibited except ISBB online sports betting and purely social gaming; courts extended the pre-internet Penal Law to remote gambling. Enforcement tools: ISP blocking under a 2017 Act (first blocks late 2018), Bank of Israel Procedure 411 restricting payments to unlicensed gambling, gambling offences are money-laundering "source offences." "B2B online gambling licences are not available in Israel." — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/gaming-law-2025/israel/law-and-practice)
- LAW/TAX: Winnings exempt up to ILS 33,840 (2024-indexed), 35% above ILS 67,680; 2018 Supreme Court and Tel Aviv District Court held professional poker winnings are taxed as regular income with tournament expenses deductible. — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/gaming-law-2025/israel/law-and-practice)
- LAW (pending): A private member's bill to regulate real-money poker tournaments was introduced (Chambers gives Nov 2021 / Dec 2022) and "awaits a decision on whether to be removed from the agenda or sent to committee." No 2024 or 2025 legislative or enforcement action is listed; expected trend is "more restrictive bills, more enforcement and ISP blocking." — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/gaming-law-2025/israel/law-and-practice)
- PRACTICE/POLITICS (2021-12-07): Knesset Education, Culture and Sport Committee debated recognising poker as a sport; MK Sharren Haskel (New Hope) reintroduced her bill (first filed in the 20th Knesset). The Israeli Poker Players Association (chair Stas Tishkevich, CEO Shimon Yafeh, legal adviser Adv. Itamar Glazer) crowdfunded ~NIS 250,000 for the effort. Haskel: "Poker has become an illegal gambling game in Israel, but it is a game that requires skill." — [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/news/politics/article-882353)
- ADVOCACY (undated, Israeli Poker Players Association, Reg. 580571875): cites Penal Law ss.224-235; criminal rulings classifying Texas Hold'em as prohibited: Rocky (2008), Araki (Magistrate's and District Court, 2015); Roni Menashe (District Court 2019) where the court noted the need for a government review; quotes Justice Wilner (Rafi Amit case) that the Supreme Court "has not yet addressed poker's classification in criminal law." States Israeli players use "unregulated online platforms" and proposes a state-run online poker site. — [IPPA document hosted on Knesset file server](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/24/Committees/24_cs_bg_613204.pdf)
- PRACTICE: The affiliate WorldPokerDeals describes Upoker as "strongest in Asia, especially for Israeli, Indian, and Chinese players," indicating an active Israeli club-app player base. — [WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/rakeback-deals/club-poker-app)
- Historical enforcement pattern: Israeli police actions reported by the Jerusalem Post have targeted operators of online gambling sites and betting rings and underground gambling venues, not individual players of foreign apps (search results: "Cops bust Israeli gambling internet site," "7 arrested on suspicion of running illegal online gambling ring," Haifa underground raid). — [Jerusalem Post search results](https://m.jpost.com/israel/cops-bust-israeli-gambling-internet-site)

### Inferences
- The legal exposure of the *tool* in Israel is indirect: the statute's ancillary-services concept exists, but every located description frames it around venues and operators; a study/review product that never touches money and does not connect players to games is not described as regulated by any source found. The realistic risks are (a) reputational/banking (Israeli banks and payment processors apply Procedure 411 to gambling-linked merchants, and a poker-branded SaaS could trip merchant-category screening), and (b) marketing: content that directs users to specific unlicensed clubs/agents moves closer to "organising/facilitating."
- Selling an Israeli-made tool primarily to Israelis who play on unlicensed apps is lawful-looking on the sources found but untested; the IPPA's own framing ("selective enforcement by individual police officers or judges") underlines the uncertainty.
- Timing: no 2024-2026 liberalisation has occurred; the poker-as-sport bill is stalled, so no near-term regulated Israeli online poker product exists to partner with.

### Gaps
- No Israeli court decision or Privacy/Consumer regulator action about poker *software* (trackers, solvers, trainers) was found; no source addresses whether selling training software constitutes "ancillary services."
- No 2024-2026 Hebrew-press coverage of Israeli enforcement against club-app players or agents was found in the searches run; absence of coverage is not evidence of absence.
- Could not confirm the current (2026) status of the Haskel poker bill beyond Chambers' "pending."

## Key Question 3: Other key markets for club apps (Brazil, Philippines, India, US, UK/EU)

### Takeaway
Market law diverged sharply in 2025-2026: India outlawed all real-money online games including poker (August 2025), while Brazil's October 2026 ban on fixed-odds betting explicitly left peer-to-peer poker open because Brazilian law treats poker as skill. Club apps themselves are unlicensed everywhere (PokerBros "is not licensed by any recognized legal body") and have exited specific US states (Minnesota, 2020). No precedent of a tracker/training-tool maker facing legal trouble in any of these markets was found; the only legal actions against tool makers located were civil actions by poker sites over data mining (PTR, c. 2010).

### Cited Findings
- India (LAW, 2025-08-22): The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Bill 2025 passed both houses and received assent; "the ban extends across online poker, rummy, fantasy sports, and other skill-based games played for real money." PokerBaazi, PokerStars India, Junglee Poker, Adda52, Natural8, Spartan Poker, MPL Poker went dark. Commencement by notification; the article does not say whether ancillary software is covered. — [Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/latest-news/2025/8/indias-black-friday-online-poker-sites-go-dark/); corroborated by [PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2025/08/black-friday-for-indian-poker-sites-halt-deposits-in-wake-of-49495.htm)
- India (PRACTICE): Nazara terminated a INR 15.9 crore PokerBaazi stake purchase after the ban. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/gaming-news/nazara-terminates-%E2%82%B915-9-crore-pokerbaazi-stake-purchase-after-online-money-gaming-ban-79948.htm)
- Brazil (LAW/PRACTICE, 2026-10-08): Provisional Measure 1.394/2026, effective 2026-10-06, prohibits operating, offering, intermediating and advertising fixed-odds betting (sports betting, online casino). "Brazilian law treats poker as a game of skill rather than gambling, so peer-to-peer poker falls outside the ban"; PokerStars said its P2P poker "is not covered by the measure"; GGPoker continues; poker rooms "never needed the fixed-odds licence." Some Brazilian players reported access problems possibly from ISP restrictions. — [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/news/online-poker-stays-open-in-brazil-despite-new-gambling-ban)
- Brazil market (PRACTICE): Brazil's "poker boom" was a SiGMA Americas 2025 panel topic. — [SiGMA](https://sigma.world/news/brazils-poker-boom-discussed-at-sigma-americas-2025/)
- USA (PRACTICE, 2020-08/09): PokerBros was removed from the US Apple App Store on 2020-08-28 for "unexpected circumstances," distributed via Apple enterprise sideloading on 2020-08-29, and restored 2020-09-05. Separately it geo-blocked Minnesota, citing the state's unclear online-poker status. — [WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/blog/pokerbros-left-minnesota-and-was-removed-from-the-us-app-store)
- USA/UK/Australia (PRACTICE, review updated 2026-07-25): PokerBros "is not licensed by any recognized legal body," is structured as a play-money platform ("legal on a technicality"), "bypasses anti-gambling laws in the US, UK, and Australia," and "the sale of chips through clubs is illegal in practice." PokerBros is "the country's biggest app" in the USA; PPPoker "now focuses on Latin America, Europe, and Australia." — [PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/pokerbros-app-review); [WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/rakeback-deals/club-poker-app)
- Licensed alternatives (PRACTICE): KKPoker and Pokio are described as a "newer generation of 'legal' apps" with in-app cashiers, versus unlicensed club apps where "players must trust their agent." — [WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/rakeback-deals/club-poker-app)
- Tracker-maker legal precedent (PRACTICE, c. 2010): PokerStars' legal action against Poker Table Ratings forced removal of player profiles; HHSmithy continued "at least until it is shown that what we are doing is illegal." These were site-vs-vendor civil disputes, not state prosecutions. — [Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/news/media-and-software/poker-edge-removes-all-public-profiles/)

### Inferences
- A companion app that does not take or move money sits outside every gambling statute surveyed; the exposure, if any, is under advertising/facilitation language (India's law and Brazil's MP both include "advertising/intermediating" verbs for the banned products) if the app promotes specific clubs or agents. India is now effectively closed as a real-money poker market, Brazil remains open for poker specifically.
- Where the club app itself is sideloaded (PokerBros iOS 2020), players are already accustomed to non-store distribution, which lowers the friction of a PWA for this audience.

### Gaps
- No source found on the Philippines' treatment of club apps or companion tools.
- No source found on UK/EU regulators' view of poker training/tracking software; the UK Gambling Commission and EU national regulators were not covered by the located material.
- No case of a tracker or training tool maker being prosecuted or fined by a state regulator in any market was found.

## Key Question 4: Apple App Store and Google Play policy for gambling-adjacent apps

### Takeaway
Apple's rule 5.3.4 targets apps that "offer real money gaming" and bans "illegal gambling aids, including card counters"; Google Play bans "support or companion functionality" for gambling (e.g. odds trackers) only when tied to gambling promotion, and requires licences only for apps that enable gambling. A no-money poker study tool fits neither definition, but both stores have histories of over-broad removals (Apple's August 2018 purge hit simulated card games and unrelated apps), Apple requires regulated-field apps to come from a legal entity rather than an individual, and existing poker trackers (Pokerbase, Bink, VPIP tracker) live on the App Store rated 18+.

### Cited Findings
- Apple (POLICY, current guidelines fetched 2026-11-05): "5.3.4 Apps that offer real money gaming (such as sports betting, poker, casino games, horse racing) or lotteries must have necessary licensing and permissions in the locations where the app is used, must be geo-restricted to those locations, and must be free on the App Store. Illegal gambling aids, including card counters, are not permitted on the App Store." 5.3.3 bans IAP for real-money gaming credit. Section 5.3 has no text on simulated gambling or age ratings. — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple (POLICY): "5.1.1(ix) Apps that provide services in highly regulated fields (such as banking and financial services, healthcare, gambling, legal cannabis use...) ... should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer." 2.3.6 requires honest age-rating answers. — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple (PRACTICE, 2018-08): Apple stopped accepting gambling apps from individual developers; the purge swept up simulated card/dice games (e.g. a long-standing Blackjack 21 app), a gif app, a video-clip app and a Polish magazine app; most non-gambling apps were restored, card/dice games less so; only incorporated businesses may now submit gambling apps. — [Gummicube](https://www.gummicube.com/blog/apple-purges-gambling-apps); corroborated by [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2018/08/09/apps-caught-up-in-app-store-ban-gambling-apps/)
- Apple (PRACTICE): PokerBros was removed from the US App Store 2020-08-28 and restored 2020-09-05 with no public reason beyond "unexpected circumstances." — [WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/blog/pokerbros-left-minnesota-and-was-removed-from-the-us-app-store)
- Apple (PRACTICE, fetched 2026-11-05): Poker tracking apps currently listed: Pokerbase - Tracking & Staking (18+, 187 ratings, 4.9), Bink Poker Bankroll Tracker, VPIP Poker Tracker, StackTrack, HandRecorder, Pokerbase AI Coach. ClubGG Poker itself is listed at 18+ with "Simulated Gambling (frequent)." — [Pokerbase listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pokerbase-tracking-staking/id1387987786); [ClubGG listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1529839330); [search results](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bink-poker-bankroll-tracker/id6445878829)
- Google Play (POLICY, fetched 2026-11-05): Gambling apps are those that "enable or facilitate online gambling"; developers must pass Google's gambling application, hold a licence per country/state, geo-block, be free, not use Play Billing, be rated AO/IARC equivalent and age-gate. Apps "may not provide gambling or real-money game, lottery, or tournament support or companion functionality," with examples including "sports score/odds/performance tracking"; the listed violation is "a dedicated sports odds tracker app containing integrated gambling ads linking to a sports betting site." Simulated-gambling apps may not carry gambling ads. The policy "doesn't say whether a standalone tracker with no gambling ads needs an application." — [Google Play Developer Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)

### Inferences
- A study/review tool that handles no money, has no IAP for chips, and does not link to clubs/agents does not meet Apple 5.3.4 or Google's gambling-app definition. The residual store risks are: being read as a "gambling aid" (Apple names only card counters, but the wording "illegal gambling aids" is open-ended), reviewer confusion from poker branding, and Google's "companion functionality" clause if the app promotes or links to clubs.
- Publishing from an incorporated entity (not an individual Apple developer account) is effectively mandatory given 5.1.1(ix) and the 2018 precedent.
- A PWA/web app avoids store review entirely and matches an audience already used to sideloading (PokerBros 2020), but gives up store discovery; nothing in the located sources shows a tracker being removed, so the store path is viable with an 18+ rating.

### Gaps
- No documented case of a poker tracker or study app being removed or restricted by Apple or Google was found; the 2018 purge concerned simulated gambling games and false positives, and PokerBros is an operator app.
- Regional availability rules for 18+ "simulated gambling" apps (e.g. country-level blocking in Korea/China) were not researched.

## Key Question 6: Go-to-market channels for club-poker players

### Takeaway
Club-app players are acquired through a layered human network: app vendor -> union -> club owner -> agent -> player, with affiliates (e.g. WorldPokerDeals, operating since 2013 with "30+ clubs," "10 professional affiliates," and 40-70% rakeback offers) negotiating directly with club owners because the apps "don't pay significant referral fees." Agents are the player-facing layer (deposits, withdrawals, rakeback, support), making them the natural distribution partner for a companion tool; no case study of a poker study tool that grew specifically through clubs or affiliates was found.

### Cited Findings
- Structure (2023-02-19, page references 2026): clubs set a real-money value on virtual chips; there is no built-in cashier; players send money to an agent who loads chips; withdrawals reverse via the agent "usually for a small fee"; payments via Skrill/Neteller or crypto (filter shows Bitcoin 10 results, USDT 9, Visa/Skrill/Neteller 0); "anyone a club owner trusts can become an agent"; clubs join unions "like poker networks." — [WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/rakeback-deals/club-poker-app)

### Inferences
- Agents and affiliates are the only actors with direct, trusted, recurring contact with club players and an economic interest in player longevity (rakeback downline). ## Key Question 7: Data and privacy (screenshots and hand histories with other players' usernames)

### Takeaway
Israel's Privacy Protection Law Amendment 13 took effect 14 August 2025 and moves Israel toward GDPR-style obligations (DPO requirement for some organisations, grace period on DPO enforcement to 31 October 2025, PPA sanctions with or without warning); the located sources did not answer whether poker usernames are personal data under Israeli law. The real precedent is contractual rather than regulatory: poker sites have historically moved against public opponent databases (PTR/Poker-Edge), and the 2026 Jurojin breach shows that stored play data on devices is now a security and reputational liability.

### Cited Findings
- LAW (effective 2025-08-14): Amendment 13 to the Privacy Protection Law 1981 took effect; certain organisations must appoint a DPO (enforcement grace period to 2025-10-31); the PPA Commissioner said some sanctions may follow a regulatory warning while "others could be imposed without warning"; the stated aim is to bring Israel closer to standards "such as the GDPR." The source did not cover registration thresholds, data minimisation, breach notification, fine amounts, or the definition of personal data. — [Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/israel-significant-amendment-to-the-privacy-law-takes-effect/)
- Additional secondary coverage of Amendment 13 exists (not fetched): Safetica, Mine, Clym, BigID, Mondaq, Vixio, Afik Law. — [search results](https://www.mondaq.com/privacy-protection/1709950/major-amendment-to-privacy-law-in-israel)
- PRACTICE (c. 2010): PokerStars' legal action forced Poker Table Ratings to remove player profiles and move to opt-in; Poker-Edge removed public player search pre-emptively; framed as unfair-advantage/data-mining enforcement by the site, not as privacy regulation. — [Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/news/media-and-software/poker-edge-removes-all-public-profiles/)
- PRACTICE (2026-10): After its update was weaponised to expose players' screens, Jurojin removed periodic hand saving "so less play data is stored on users' PCs." — [PokerNews](https://pokernews.com/news/2026/10/jurojin-speaks-after-superuser-scandal-52575.htm)
- POLICY: Apple 4.7.1 and 5.1 require privacy compliance for data collection; Google Play's gambling policy requires responsible-gambling information for gambling apps (not applicable to a no-money tool). — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)

### Inferences
- Screenshots containing opponents' club usernames are at most pseudonymous identifiers; the located Israeli sources do not settle whether they are "personal data," but a GDPR-style reading (which Amendment 13 approximates) would treat online identifiers as personal data when linkable. Practical mitigations implied by the sources: redact/hash opponent names on ingestion, keep per-user data siloed (no cross-user opponent database, which is the feature poker sites have historically attacked), minimise stored play data, and secure the update/supply chain.
- The regulatory risk appears modest relative to the platform/contract risk: a cross-player opponent database would reproduce exactly the data-mining pattern that drew PokerStars' legal action and that all major sites prohibit.

### Gaps
- No Israeli PPA guidance or decision on gaming/poker data, usernames, or screenshot processing was found.
- The Amendment 13 details most relevant to a small startup (database registration threshold, DPO trigger criteria, fine ceilings, breach-notification rules) were not confirmed from the fetched source and should be pulled from the statute or PPA guidance.
- No GDPR enforcement action against a poker tracker or hand-history service was found.
