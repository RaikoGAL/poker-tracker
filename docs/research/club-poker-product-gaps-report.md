# Build Club Poker's Missing Discipline Ledger

The player this app serves is a mobile-only, agent-dependent cash-game player who cannot verify anything about their own poker life — whether the agent will pay, whether the table is clean, what rake and rakeback they really paid, or whether they are winning — and no shipping product gives them a trustworthy record of any of it. Bankroll trackers are live-poker ledgers, HUDs and solvers are Windows products that need hand-history files club apps never write, and the only software built for club apps serves agents and bot-runners; **not one product combines bankroll discipline with hand review**. The improvement evidence says results are statistically meaningless at recreational volumes (a 5 bb/100 winner has roughly a **44% chance of losing any 1,000-hand sample**), so the app must measure process — self-labeled mistake rates, late-session error clustering, limit adherence — and show win rate only inside a confidence band. On behavior change, field trials favor limits set as a mandatory step before play, self-authored rather than suggested, enforced by friction at the moment of a rebuy or deposit, with forgiving weekly streaks; generic pop-ups, pushed statements and persuasion copy have no field support and one measured backfire. The constraints are real but navigable: ClubGG's June 2026 terms ban automation and non-interface access but never mention screenshots, yet **ClubGG shipped "Screen Guard" to iOS on 21 October 2026**, six days after GGPoker's blanket third-party-software ban, and nobody yet knows whether it blacks out the replay screen. Hands can be captured today through post-session share-sheet screenshots parsed by a vision model with pot-math validation, with X-Poker replay links and PokerBros' emailed exports as cleaner structured paths. The ranked opportunity is a multi-currency, agent-aware ledger with friction-enforced precommitment first, process-metric review second and AI hand analysis third; the biggest risks are Screen Guard, club-level hostility to anything that reads the table, and the untested assumption that recreational players will log anything at all.

## The target player is a mobile-only, agent-dependent cash player who can verify nothing

Nobody knows how many people play real-money poker in app clubs, and that is by design. The apps describe themselves as play-money or social products, money settles off-platform through agents, and even a 2026 industry round-up that publishes concurrency for every regulated network (PokerStars ~60,000 concurrent cash players, GGPoker ~50,000) says club volume is "difficult to quantify precisely" ([WeeBet via DEV Community](https://dev.to/weebet/state-of-online-poker-2026-the-year-in-numbers-4b10)). The best proxies are PPPoker's own 2019 claim of "several million downloads" and "thousands of clubs" across 100+ countries ([CardPlayer](https://cardplayer.com/poker-news/24502-pppoker-is-the-app-for-poker-lovers)) and union-level table counts: the largest ClubGG union, Massiv, runs roughly 100 clubs and 200+ ring tables at North American evening peak, with Taiwan-based Tiny Union at 150–180 tables ([ThePokerAgent](https://thepokeragent.com/biggest-unions-on-poker-apps/)). Geography splits by platform — PPPoker in Asia and the CIS, PokerBros in North America, Latin America and Europe, ClubGG the most globally distributed ([PokerNet blog](https://pokernetai.pages.dev/blog/pppoker-vs-pokerbros-vs-clubgg-operational-comparison/)) — and the ecosystem is a long tail of small clubs paying $20–50 a month in platform fees ([PokerNet blog](https://pokernetai.pages.dev/blog/pppoker-vs-pokerbros-vs-clubgg-operational-comparison/)). The honest inference is an addressable population in the hundreds of thousands of monthly-active real-money players globally, spread across thousands of clubs, which means a tool must follow a player who moves between clubs and unions rather than assume one "site."

Four segments emerge from forum and review evidence, and they are distinct customers. The recreational loser deposits "a couple of hundred" through an agent they have never met, often via a referral, and never tallies the total; the CardsChat thread where a PokerBros club "just vanishes" and the agent goes silent for five weeks is the archetype — "You have to put all of your trust in one single person" ([CardsChat via casino.us](https://www.casino.us/cardschat/poker-rooms-10/pokerbros-club-chaos-need-help-512977/)). The aspiring semi-pro studies and wants stats but hits a wall: on ClubGG "PokerTracker or Hold'em Manager are not available" ([VIP-Grinders](https://www.vip-grinders.com/clubgg-review)). The grinder has routed around that wall with a Windows stack — "Try Drivehud (Asian Hand Converter) + LD Player combo" ([TwoPlusTwo](https://forumserver.twoplustwo.com/292/online-poker-sites/pokerbros-discussion-thread-1757085/index153.html)) — and worries about rake and collusion rather than tooling. The agent or club owner is a fourth actor who pays roughly $5 a week for in-app reports and settles downlines weekly; agent commission runs 40–70% of downline rake and is adjusted downward by about 10% on cash when the downline wins, which tells you the business model assumes net-losing player pools ([PokerNet](https://pokernetai.pages.dev/blog/poker-club-agent-commission-structure-how-rakeback/)). Device is overwhelmingly mobile: ClubGG is "mobile-first" with a Windows client ([Ivo Manolov](https://write.as/ivo-manolov/clubgg-review-2026-traffic-games-rakeback-and-safety)), PokerBros is Android/iOS only with PC play via emulator ([PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/poker-sites/pokerbros-app-review)).

What these players struggle with, in their own words, is not strategy but not knowing. They cannot verify the agent: PokerListings reports "slow or unpaid withdrawals, including complaints of four and five-figure sums," withdrawals with "no fixed procedure," and an operator that disclaims liability ([PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/poker-sites/pokerbros-app-review)). They cannot verify the table: on r/poker a player says that on ClubGG "7 or 8 seats at a table can show the exact same VPIP," another puts bots at "at least 10%" of players, while others call the complainers "simply losing" ([r/poker via mirror](https://r.datuan.dev/r/poker/comments/1tf2iju/bots_are_taking_over/)). They cannot verify the price: rake is "theoretically 3-5%" but "club owners may charge 10-12% on some games," unions "may take up to 10%," and clubs add uncapped fees ([PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/poker-sites/pokerbros-app-review)); rakeback is paid by the agent, not the app, with documented abuses such as "rakeback paid only when you lose" and "rake minus winnings" schemes that pay a winning player nothing, so players are told to request a weekly rake statement from the agent ([RatePokerClub](https://www.ratepokerclub.com/blog/poker-rakeback-clubs)). And they cannot verify themselves: the r/poker canon on winning players is "Just a spreadsheet or even a notebook with day, buy-in amount, and cash-out amount," written by someone whose grandfather "had no clue how much he was losing" and who notes that self-described break-even players have "willingly lied to [themselves]" ([r/poker via mirror](https://lr.sudovanilla.org/r/poker/comments/8ugm6w/the_one_thing_a_winning_poker_player_does)). A club player's true result is table result minus rake plus rakeback, and two of those three terms are invisible or agent-controlled.

The base rate makes honest record-keeping urgent. Hendon Mob's analysis of two ~3.4-million-hand Full Tilt databases found roughly **two-thirds of all players are net losers**, 60% of NLHE players with 100+ hands lose, and only ~1% of all accounts reached 5,000 hands as winners ([The Hendon Mob](https://www.thehendonmob.com/?p=2381)); a micro-stakes pro's own database estimate puts long-term winners "at around 30%" ([BlackRain79](https://www.blackrain79.com/2019/09/what-percentage-of-poker-players-are.html)); and across online gambling generally, a 717,724-consumer UCSD study found only ~4% withdrew more than they deposited over 2019–2023 ([GamblingHarm.org](https://gamblingharm.org/most-online-gamblers-lose-study/)). Club rake of 5–7% with some uncapped tables is higher than regulated-site rake, so the losing share in clubs is plausibly higher still unless the player secures rakeback. The specific failure the app targets is the impulsive reload: the agent is one chat message away and chips arrive in minutes, so a stop-loss "set mid-session by a stuck player is a negotiation, not a stop-loss," and stuck players "widen opening ranges, call more river bets, bluff into calling stations, move up in stakes, and play longer sessions" ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-stop-loss-session-management-guide)).

Israel is a small but real and underserved pocket. Online poker there is unregulated, the government "penalizes credit card companies that process gambling payments," most platforms refuse shekels, and PokerStars has exited ([WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/blog/pokerstars-closed-israel-at-what-poker-rooms-may-israeli-players-play)) — exactly the conditions under which an agent model settled in ILS through local payment apps thrives. ClubGG club lobbies run shekel-denominated PLO tables at 10/20 and 20/40 ILS alongside USD games ([VIP-Grinders](https://www.vip-grinders.com/clubgg-review)), the Israeli App Store listing carries 240 ratings at 3.0/5 with reviews complaining "if you're not in the time zone of the americas then don't get it" ([Apple App Store IL](https://apps.apple.com/il/app/clubgg-poker/id1529839330)), and an affiliate names Upoker as "strongest in Asia, especially for Israeli, Indian, and Chinese players" ([WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/rakeback-deals/club-poker-app)). Multi-currency with per-club chip-to-currency rates is therefore a first-class requirement, not an edge case, and the late-night sessions an Israeli plays on an Americas union compound the fatigue and tilt risk the app exists to manage.

## Every existing tool was built for someone else

The competitive map has three stable categories and a hole in the middle. Bankroll trackers are dominated by three long-lived indie iOS apps at $20–35 a year: Poker Bankroll Tracker (4.2 from 93 US ratings, Pro $29.99/yr or $199.99 lifetime) ([Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id999514771)), Pokerbase (4.9 from 187 ratings, Pro $12.99/mo or $99.99/yr, Elite $299.99/yr) ([Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1387987786)), and Poker Analytics, whose Android build shows roughly 21,000 lifetime installs, about 407 monthly actives and under $10,000 a month in revenue ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/net.pokeranalytics.android)). Every one of them is live-poker-first — venues, trips, tips, receipt scanning, WSOP calendars — and supports online play only in the trivial sense that manual entry is platform-agnostic; a Pokerbase user had to request a way to mark online games separately from in-person ones ([Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1387987786)). Their reviews teach the real job-to-be-done: praise is about reliability and developer responsiveness ("the only bankroll tracking app I use and the only one I need"), and the complaints that drive abandonment are data loss ("Lost 2 years of data twice now"), broken backup, abandoned updates, and paying yearly for "the exact same thing" peers do for less ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/316520188/poker-income-bankroll-tracker/reviews); [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1073540690/poker-analytics-5-tracker/reviews)). Replayers, calculators and GIF export appear in feature lists but never in "why I pay" statements. The user is buying a trustworthy ledger, and the ledger is what they punish you for losing.

Hand-history trackers and HUDs are a Windows world that club apps lock out. PT4 and HM3 cost $99 one-time, DriveHUD 3 $59.99, Hand2Note $15.99–$49 a month ([WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/soft/trackers); [PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/poker-tools/trackers/hand2note)), and all of them need hand-history files that exist on club apps only if you run the app in an Android emulator on a PC and pay for a "converter" — DriveHUD's Asian Hand Converter covers 13 apps including ClubGG, PokerBros, PPPoker, X-Poker and Suprema at $29 a month, Windows only ([DriveHUD](https://drivehud.com/asianhandconverter/)), and a dedicated ClubGG converter costs $30 a month and "works only on the desktop version of the Windows client" ([GipsyTeam shop](https://shop.gipsyteam.com/clubgg-converter)). The vendors assert safety without evidence ("The ClubGG converter is fully protected from bans") ([PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/blog/hand-converter-and-mining-everything-you-need-to-know)), while Hand2Note has to ship ClubGG-specific patches after each client update ([Hand2Note Telegram](https://t.me/s/Hand2Note?after=316)) and PokerBros can detect emulator use and let club owners hide games from emulator players ([Professional Rakeback](https://professionalrakeback.com/poker-bros-app-review)). This stack serves PC-based regs who already have results tracking; a mobile-only ClubGG player has literally zero hand tooling, compliant or otherwise.

Solver and AI review products reach club apps only through that same stack. GTO Wizard owns the segment at $39–$289 a month with an analyzer that accepts 17 hand-history formats including PokerBros and PPPoker but not ClubGG, gated by caps of 5 free, 100 Starter and 100,000 Premium hands a month ([VIP-Grinders](https://www.vip-grinders.com/?p=143094)); its Ultra tier rose to $359 a month in October 2026 ([PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/news/promotions/2026/10/gto-wizard-ultra-early-bird-offer-last-chance-52603.htm)). On mobile, the only AI products with real rating volume are preflop quiz apps — GTO Preflop Wizard with 3,467 US ratings, Runout with 4,475 on Google Play, Postflop+ with roughly 2,000 on each store — while hand-review apps have 3 to 60 ratings ([AI Tools Bakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/?p=19102)). Drilling is proven to sell on phones; mobile hand review is not yet. Exactly two products touch screenshots: Pokerbase AI Coach added "GG screenshot analysis" on 28 September 2026 and has three ratings ([Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6777828462)), and Deepfold parses club-app screenshots and X-Poker replay links but caps users at 3, 10 or 30 hands a month across its tiers ([Deepfold](https://deepfold.co/en/blog/hand-converter-guide)), which suggests per-hand parsing is either expensive or human-checked.

The one category built for club apps serves the other side of the table. PA Tools centralizes per-player balances, rake and rakeback across clubs for agents and admins, with pricing available only through a Telegram manager ([PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/blog/pa-tools-overview-platform-for-poker-apps-agents-and-players)); converter vendors double as rakeback agents who mark their tools "Free for WPD players" ([WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/soft/hand-converters/asian-hand-converter)); and searches for ClubGG tooling are dominated by bot vendors selling "managed AI bot services" to club owners ([PokerNet](https://pokernetai.pages.dev/blog/pppoker-vs-pokerbros-vs-clubgg-operational-comparison/)). No player-facing results or study product for club platforms exists.

Discipline as a positioning exists only in near-zero-traction form. Bankroll Buddy offers "a stop-loss or a time limit you set yourself" and has about 100 installs and a 1.0-star average ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6752291028)); Discipline Poker plans sessions with time limits and post-game self-ratings but mentions no stop-loss, cooldown, deposit rule or enforcement, and went donation-only ([Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6758074740)); Final Table combines hand logging, auto-stats, AI review and bankroll tracking but has no discipline rules and under 1,000 downloads ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/final-table/6760188970)). The reason nobody has merged discipline with review is structural: trackers are live-poker ledgers whose users have no hands to review, review tools are hand-history products whose users already own PT4, mental-game content is sold as $597 courses rather than instrumented into session data ([PokerStrategy](https://www.pokerstrategy.com/news/pokerstrategy/Save-up-to-$250-at-Raise-Your-Edge!_119979/)), and on club apps neither data source arrives automatically, so a combined product has to be built on manual and screenshot entry that incumbents have no reason to attempt. The combination is untested, not disproven.

## Results are noise below 10,000 hands, so measure process instead

The single most important design constraint is variance. Online 6-max cash has a standard deviation of roughly 90 bb/100, so for a 5 bb/100 winner "noise is about 18 times the signal"; the 95% confidence interval is **±18 bb/100 at 10,000 hands**, ±5.6 at 100,000 and ±2.5 at 500,000, which means a 5 bb/100 winner with 100,000 hands still cannot rule out breakeven, and ordinary downswings for a winner run 15–25 buy-ins ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-variance-explained-sample-size-win-rate)). A coaching analysis using SD 115 finds a 5 bb/100 winner has a 44% chance of losing over 1,000 hands and that a 7.5 bb/100 winner has only a 58% chance of a winning 1,000-hand sample versus 56% for the 5 bb/100 player — a 50% improvement in true skill is invisible in session results — and therefore recommends a predetermined "Results Day" no more often than every 10,000 hands, typically 10,000–50,000 ([Mobius](https://mobiuspokercoaching.squarespace.com/blog/when-and-how-to-check-results)). Simulations show a 2.5 bb/100 winner has a 97% chance of at least one 100,000-hand unprofitable stretch within a million hands ([Mobius](https://mobiuspokercoaching.squarespace.com/blog/beyond-downswings-why-extended-breakeven-runs-are-inevitable)). A recreational club player logging 5,000 hands a month will never reach a sample where bb/100 means anything, so a dashboard built around bb/100 is a dashboard built around a random number.

Skill is nonetheless real and persistent. The largest academic dataset — 456 million player-hand observations across 611,000 accounts — found top-decile players earned about **19.5 bb/100 more** than bottom-decile players in the following six months, that a sample-size-adjusted "performance robustness measure" ranks players better than raw win rate, that the more skilled of two extreme groups leads 75% of the time only after roughly 1,500–7,000 hands depending on the grouping, and that rake averaged about 16 bb/100, leaving only 32% of players profitable after rake ([PMC4346402](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4346402)). WSOP data tells the same story: a priori high-skill players posted +30.5% ROI against −15.6% for everyone else, winning pairwise matchups 54.9% of the time ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17023/w17023.pdf)). The implication is that the app can honestly promise the player improvement is real and detectable — but only through measures that converge faster than money.

Preflop frequencies converge fastest because they generate a data point nearly every orbit; a pro explains that VPIP, PFR, RFI, 3-bet and steal "converge into a reliable sample size over the shortest period of time," while 300 hands produced only 11 c-bet opportunities, so postflop stats must be judged by opportunities rather than hands ([PokerNews / Laplante](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/teachable-moments-from-twitch-ryan-laplante-hud-stats-32348.htm)). That yields a defensible display ladder (a synthesis, not a single source): at session scale, show no win-rate judgment at all, only process data and preflop frequencies as "indicative" drift against the player's own plan; at ~1,000 hands, preflop stats by position become usable for self-consistency; at ~10,000 hands, the first Results Day with a ±18 bb/100 band and postflop stats by opportunity count; at 50,000–100,000, trends in win rate start to mean something. Win rate should always carry a confidence band and a "hands until this band is ±X" counter, which is what the performance-robustness finding implies a player-facing product should do.

What replaces win rate is the review itself, and the practitioner literature is unusually consistent about its shape. Annie Duke's "resulting" — "too tight a relationship between the quality of the outcome and the quality of the decision" — gives the core rule: never evaluate a decision by leading with its outcome, and when seeking feedback, describe the hand but withhold your decision and the result so the listener is not anchored ([Nautilus](https://nautil.us/the-resulting-fallacy-is-ruining-your-decisions-236901)). Training-site routines operationalize it: review within 24 hours "while your reasoning is still recoverable," limit to 10–15 flagged hands plus the largest losing and winning pots, answer the same five questions for each (opponent's range per street, goal of the action, sizing fit, range consistency, whether the result swayed judgment), categorize each mistake and add a tally mark, tag errors technical versus mental, and note that "if leaks cluster late in sessions or after bad beats, the fix is emotional management, not more study" ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-session-review-process-guide-2026)). Hands should be labeled outcome-independently as mistake, cooler or unclear, with the error located at "the street where the error began, not just where the chips went in" ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-hand-history-review-routine-find-leaks-2026)). Jared Tendler's process model adds the cheapest high-adoption data: set one process goal before sitting down, rate play and mindset 1–10 afterward, fix one problem at a time, and never analyze while playing ([TMGOP notes](https://alexspuffstuff.blogspot.com/2016/07/tmgop-notes.html?m=1)). His "inchworm" says improvement comes from raising the worst of your range, not only the best, and progress should be judged "worst to your previous worst" ([Hendon Mob](https://www.thehendonmob.com/?p=1929)). Study-to-play ratio advice — 50:50 for novices down to 20:80 for advanced players — is practitioner opinion with no controlled evidence ([BlackRain79](https://www.blackrain79.com/2016/11/study-versus-play-poker.html?m=1)), and should be treated as a nudge, not a metric.

Tilt research turns this review into a mental-game instrument. Sensitivity to losses — feelings of injustice and anger after bad beats — is the strongest predictor of tilt severity (B=0.598), and, against expectation, more experience predicts *more* severe tilt (B=0.22) unless the player perceives that experience has taught them to tilt less, in which case severity drops sharply (B=−0.48) ([Palomäki dissertation](https://core.ac.uk/download/17340392.pdf)). Experience does not fix tilt; recognizing one's own patterns does. Problem-risk players report tilting about 70% more often than low-risk players (3.8 vs 2.2 on the scale), and players regard identifying tilt and stopping play as the only adequate response ([Moreau et al.](https://cdspress.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Moreau-et-al..pdf)). That argues for a "tilt profile" that shows the player their own triggers and late-session error clustering rather than a mood emoji, and for cooldown prompts keyed to large lost pots, which a hand log makes detectable automatically. The bankroll layer closes the loop quantitatively: lifetime risk of ruin follows RoR = e^(−2Bμ/σ²), a 3 bb/100 winner at SD 90 needs about 62 buy-ins for 1% ruin, and the same 40-buy-in roll goes from ~2% lifetime ruin at 4 bb/100 to **37% at 1 bb/100** — the "tilt tax" made concrete ([DeucesCracked RoR study](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/poker-risk-of-ruin-variance-simulation-study)). Because true win rate is unknowable at these samples, bankroll health should be computed from a conservative lower-confidence-bound win rate, and the common move-down rules (drop below 20 buy-ins, after a 15-buy-in downswing, cap shots at 4 buy-ins) ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/cash-game-bankroll-management-2026-rules-traffic-light-system)) should be presented as conventions, since no field study tests them.

Two cautions temper the enthusiasm for structure. A randomized trial of a structured reasoning method with professional intelligence analysts "failed to improve accuracy," while aggregating about three independent judgments did ([Mandel et al. 2018](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/1530E9DAE8F42B2E8FC2B5FE374DB50F/S1930297500006628a.pdf/boosting-intelligence-analysts-judgment-accuracy-what-works-what-fails.pdf)), so a heavyweight review template is not automatically better than a tag-and-tally baseline plus a second opinion (solver, partner or AI), and templates should be A/B tested. And the central product hypothesis — that a falling self-labeled mistake rate predicts later win rate — is untested anywhere in the literature; the app would be generating the first evidence.

## Friction at the moment of action works; pop-ups, statements and persuasion do not

The operator literature on precommitment is the closest analog to a poker discipline app, and it is consistent on three points. First, voluntary limits are taken up by a small minority: in Bet365's randomized trials with the Behavioural Insights Team, about 4% of 45,000 invited customers set a deposit limit, and adding a "self-persuasion" prompt or a "personal commitment" prompt with SMS reminders *lowered* uptake from 4.4% to 3.6% and 2.9% ([Auer & Griffiths commentary](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/45868/1/1524718_Griffiths.pdf)). Persuasion copy is not the lever; defaults and placement are. Second, limits work when set: among 49,560 Kindred players, the most intense gamblers who set voluntary limits spent significantly less a year later ([NTU IRep](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/38900)), and in win2day's 100,000-player sample **poker players' spending "significantly decreased after setting a voluntary time limit,"** with the largest drop in playing duration of any game type — a result that argues for a session time cap alongside the stop-loss ([Journal of Gambling Studies](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10899-012-9332-y.pdf)). Third, people route around limits: 68% of Swedish self-excluders gambled during exclusion ([JMIR Mental Health](https://mental.jmir.org/2022/8/e37837)), and under Germany's €1,000 cap, 42% of self-reported cap-hitters kept gambling elsewhere while 60.5% believed they had hit the cap when only 7.6% actually had ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10899-022-10155-1)). A non-operator app cannot block a cashier at all, so leakage is the central risk, and off-plan deposits must be a tracked event the player is invited to log, not a hidden failure state.

These results translate into specific design rules. Make limit-setting a mandatory step of starting a session rather than a settings page, in the way win2day requires limits at account opening ([Journal of Gambling Studies](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10899-012-9332-y.pdf)). Let the player author the number: Bet365's anchoring trial found chosen limits were roughly 45% lower with a low-anchor dropdown or free text than with a high-anchor menu ([Auer & Griffiths commentary](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/45868/1/1524718_Griffiths.pdf)), so default to free text or anchor on the player's own bankroll data (two to three buy-ins). Alert at 80% of the player's *own* limit — Norsk Tipping's personalized "you have passed 80% of your monthly loss limit" message, received by 7,884 of 54,002 players, was followed by significant reductions in money gambled ([NTU IRep](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/35133)). And make loosening slow and tightening instant: Germany's cap increases take effect only the following month ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10899-022-10155-1)), and Beeminder's "akrasia horizon" delays any goal change by a week so you cannot loosen it in the moment ([Beeminder blog](https://blog.beeminder.com/derail)). A raised stop-loss or deposit cap should apply after 24 hours to 7 days; a lowered one immediately.

The enforcement mechanism available to a non-operator app is friction, and friction has the best field evidence of anything here. The one sec app, tested in a six-week PNAS field experiment with 280 participants, inserts a short wait plus a one-tap "don't open" exit when a user opens a target app: users abandoned 36% of attempts and actual opens fell **57% after six weeks**; a 500-person follow-up found the dismiss option had the strongest effect and the delay also worked, while the reflective message alone was not effective ([PNAS abstract via BVS](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/ses/resource/pt/mdl-36795756)). The poker translation is direct: when a player logs a rebuy past stop-loss or taps "deposit," show a 10–20 second enforced wait and a large one-tap "Stop, end session" exit; the "write your reason" text field is secondary and optional, logged for the next-day review rather than relied on to persuade. Implementation intentions — "if I lose three buy-ins, then I close the app and walk" — have a medium effect overall (d≈0.65) but are weaker for highly impulsive people and in document-only delivery ([Wang, Wang & Gai 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/pdf)), which describes a tilting player reading a form, so they belong in a guided, conversational pre-session plan set while calm, and should be flexible rather than rigid.

What backfires is equally clear. In Danish randomized operator trials with 14,153 players, hourly reality checks showing time played and net result, "healthy habits" messages and fortnightly activity statements produced no significant change in spend, and a high-activity warning that prompted a self-test *increased* casino turnover by 8.9% (p=0.02) ([Danish Competition and Consumer Authority](https://kfst.dk/media/rykbo5rn/20231219-do-behaviorally-informed-pop-up-messages-curb-problematic-gambling-behavior.pdf)). Pushed dashboards and periodic pop-ups should not be the core of this product; in-session triggers should key off logged loss events, which tilt research identifies as the real trigger, not elapsed time. Guilt mechanics have no direct test, but the indirect evidence — Duolingo's forgiveness features won while harder imposed goals did not, and gamblers call mandatory limits patronizing ([Auer & Griffiths commentary](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/45868/1/1524718_Griffiths.pdf)) — says a broken stop-loss should be logged neutrally with a next step, never scolded. Because iOS push opt-in in financial services ran about 55% even in 2014 data ([Andrew Chen / Kahuna](https://andrewchen.com/why-people-are-turning-off-push/amp)), retention cannot depend on notifications, and permission should be requested only after the player has set a first session plan.

Retention mechanics should be borrowed from Duolingo and adapted to a weekly cadence. Across 600+ streak experiments, two streak freezes beat one and three were no better, giving *new* users two freezes up front was one of the biggest wins, an "earn back" path for lost streaks helped without cheapening the streak, the 1-to-2-day transition produced a large retention jump with habit "locked in" around day 7, user-chosen goals with an explicit opt-out beat pre-selected harder goals, and changing a button from "Continue" to "Commit to my goal" was a significant win ([Recall summary](https://www.recall.it/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team); [Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks)). A player who sits three to five sessions a week needs a Peloton-style weekly unit — "respected my stop-loss every session this week" — not a daily check-in, and the first-week goal should be trivially small and self-chosen. Logging friction is the churn engine to design against: in a six-month pilot, manual dietary logging adherence fell from about 70% in week one to 22%, with a median time to disengagement of 10 weeks, while passively captured activity stayed consistent far longer — yet the same paper cites studies where fully passive capture produced *weaker* habit formation and outcomes than light manual entry, concluding "some burden may be needed for behavior change" ([JMIR Formative Research](https://formative.jmir.org/2022/2/e33603)). The target is a three-tap session log with smart defaults and a deliberate "I confirm this result" tap, deferred detail in a next-day review, and aggressive design for return after lapse, since 65% of lapsed weight-loggers in that study re-engaged. Benchmarks suggest aiming at finance-app retention (about 30% day-1 and 12% day-30) rather than health apps (about 20% and 3–4%) ([Plotline](https://www.plotline.so/blog/retention-rates-mobile-apps-by-industry)).

## ClubGG's terms are vague, its Screen Guard is real, and the stores are passable

ClubGG's public Terms of Service, last updated 17 June 2026 and contracting with NSUS Group Inc, contain no clause naming HUDs, trackers, screenshots, screen capture, data mining, hand-history export or real-time assistance. The operative restrictions are generic: members may not "use automated technology or otherwise unfairly alter your likelihood of winning," may not use "automated technology, including but not limited to robots, scripts, macros, and/or programs," may not "modify, translate, reverse engineer, decompile, disassemble or create derivative works," and agree "not to access the Site by any means other than through the interface" ([ClubGG ToS](https://www.clubgg.com/poker-terms-of-service)). The words "capture," "record," "screenshot," "tool" and "assist" do not appear in any user-conduct restriction. The document reads as a generic NSUS contest template and incorporates "posted guidelines, policies" by reference, so NSUS could impose a GGPoker-style software policy without amending it, and compliance decisions are "final and binding and not subject to challenge or appeal." The inference — not a statement of enforcement — is that a tool that scrapes the client or injects actions falls under these clauses, while a tool that consumes screenshots the user takes manually after the session is not addressed by any clause found.

The secondary sources on the other apps conflict and are all second-hand. PokerListings says PokerBros' terms "allow only the PokerBros HUD and prohibit other external tools" ([PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/poker-sites/pokerbros-app-review)), while Professional Rakeback says "The terms do not prohibit HUD and tracking software such as Holdem Manager and Poker Tracker... Solvers and real time assistance software are against the rules," and that an agent's whole downline can be banned for the agent's actions ([Professional Rakeback](https://professionalrakeback.com/poker-bros-app-review)). PPPoker added a term in 2021 prohibiting "simulators and third-party software," with the trade press noting "no precedents of sanctions against players for third-party software" and that enforcement "may depend on individual club administrators" ([WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/blog/software-ban-and-other-new-features-from-the-pppoker-security-team)). No documented 2024–2026 ban for post-session tooling on any club app was found; enforcement coverage concerns collusion and bots. The practical power sits with club owners and agents, so the risk is reputational at the club level more than a vendor ban.

The industry mood has nonetheless hardened sharply since September 2026. A tampered update of the Jurojin table-management tool installed spyware that let an attacker view players' screens, with one player believing he lost at least $100,000 ([PokerNews](https://pokernews.com/news/2026/10/jurojin-speaks-after-superuser-scandal-52575.htm)). On 15 October 2026 GGPoker announced a "New Policy: Banning of all 3rd Party Poker Software and Automation Tools," stating "Previously, we permitted certain third-party tools that did not affect gameplay," naming PokerTracker 4, Hand2Note, Holdem Manager 3, DriveHUD 2, Equilab and AutoHotkey among prohibited tools, and introducing four optional client-security features including Screen Guard, which "keeps your tables out of screenshots, recordings, screen shares, and remote-viewing apps," with a "Protect the whole Client" option extending to lobby, cashier and account pages; "Every feature is optional and runs only while GGPoker is open" ([GGPoker](https://legal.ggpoker.com/blog/new-security-measures-at-ggpoker/)). Automated detection and an in-client warning system go live on 1 December 2026 ([TightPoker](https://www.tightpoker.com/news/ggpoker-december-1-enforcement-date-third-party-software-ban/)). The official post and every secondary report found say nothing about ClubGG. But Apple's version history for ClubGG Poker shows **version 26.06.0239 on 21 October 2026 with the release note "Added Screen Guard"** ([Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/clubgg-poker/id1529839330)) — six days after GGPoker's announcement, and strong circumstantial evidence that NSUS ported the feature. No source describes what ClubGG's Screen Guard covers, whether it is on by default, whether it reaches Android or Windows, or — the question that matters most for this product — whether it blacks out the hand-history and replay screen or only live tables. The Google Play listing fetched on 5 November still showed an August update ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nsus.clubgg&hl=en)). ClubGG's own store copy promises "detailed game history and statistics for games played" without using the words "hand history," "replay" or "export" ([Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/clubgg-poker/id1529839330)), and secondary coverage describes paid "Smart HUD" and "PokerCraft" features under $9.99 and $49.99 memberships ([PokerListings ClubGG](https://www.pokerlistings.com/poker-sites/clubgg)), so NSUS has a commercial interest in being the only stats provider.

Israeli law constrains the player more than the tool. Under Penal Law Chapter 12, criminal courts have classified poker as a "prohibited game"; organizing carries up to 3 years or ILS 452,000 and playing up to 1 year or ILS 29,200; online gambling is prohibited outside the state sports monopoly; ISP blocking began in 2018 and Bank of Israel Procedure 411 restricts payments to unlicensed gambling; the Supreme Court's 2018 Amit ruling suggested poker may be skill-based for a professional, but only in obiter ([Chambers Gaming Law 2025](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/gaming-law-2025/israel/law-and-practice)). The poker-as-sport bill reintroduced by MK Sharren Haskel remains stalled ([Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/news/politics/article-882353)). No Israeli decision or regulator action about poker *software* was found; the statute's ancillary-services concept is described around venues and operators, and reported police actions target site operators and gambling rings, not players of foreign apps ([Jerusalem Post](https://m.jpost.com/israel/cops-bust-israeli-gambling-internet-site)). The realistic exposure for an Israeli-founded tool is therefore banking and merchant screening under Procedure 411, and marketing that steers users to specific unlicensed clubs or agents, which edges toward facilitation. Abroad, India outlawed all real-money online games including poker in August 2025 ([Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/latest-news/2025/8/indias-black-friday-online-poker-sites-go-dark/)), while Brazil's October 2026 ban on fixed-odds betting explicitly left peer-to-peer poker open because Brazilian law treats it as skill ([Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/news/online-poker-stays-open-in-brazil-despite-new-gambling-ban)) — so Brazil, a major ClubGG base, is a live expansion market and India is closed.

App-store policy is passable with care. Apple's rule 5.3.4 targets apps that "offer real money gaming" and bans "illegal gambling aids, including card counters," and 5.1.1(ix) requires apps in regulated fields such as gambling to be "submitted by a legal entity... and not by an individual developer" ([Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)); Apple's August 2018 purge swept up simulated card games and unrelated apps before most were restored ([Gummicube](https://www.gummicube.com/blog/apple-purges-gambling-apps)), and PokerBros itself was pulled for a week in 2020 ([WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/blog/pokerbros-left-minnesota-and-was-removed-from-the-us-app-store)). Google Play bans gambling "support or companion functionality" only in the context of gambling promotion, with its example a sports-odds tracker carrying betting ads ([Google Play Developer Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)). Pokerbase, Bink, VPIP Tracker and StackTrack all live on the App Store rated 18+ ([Pokerbase listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pokerbase-tracking-staking/id1387987786)). A no-money tool published by an incorporated entity, rated 18+, with no chip IAP and no links to clubs or agents, fits neither store's gambling definition; a PWA sidestepping review is viable for an audience already used to sideloading, at the cost of discovery.

Privacy risk is modest relative to platform risk, with one hard rule. Israel's Privacy Protection Law Amendment 13 took effect 14 August 2025 and moves the country toward GDPR-style obligations, with DPO requirements for some organizations and sanctions that may be imposed without warning ([Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/israel-significant-amendment-to-the-privacy-law-takes-effect/)); whether club usernames are personal data is unsettled in the sources, but a GDPR-style reading treats linkable online identifiers as personal data. The hard rule comes from history: PokerStars' legal action forced Poker Table Ratings to remove public player profiles and Poker-Edge pre-emptively killed player search ([Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/news/media-and-software/poker-edge-removes-all-public-profiles/)), and Jurojin's post-breach remediation removed periodic hand saving "so less play data is stored on users' PCs" ([PokerNews](https://pokernews.com/news/2026/10/jurojin-speaks-after-superuser-scandal-52575.htm)). The app must never build a cross-user opponent database: hash or redact opponent names on ingestion, silo each player's data, minimize what is stored on device, and treat the update supply chain as a security surface.

## Agents are the distribution channel, and the price sits between $13 and $39

Club players are reached through a human network, not an app store. The structure runs app vendor to union to club owner to agent to player: clubs set a real-money value on chips, there is no cashier, players send money to an agent who loads chips, and "anyone a club owner trusts can become an agent" ([WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/rakeback-deals/club-poker-app)). Affiliates negotiate directly with club owners because "apps don't pay significant referral fees," sometimes collecting deposits themselves and bearing the risk that "one missed payout could end their business" ([iGB Affiliate](https://www.igbaffiliate.com/en/articles/seo/age-of-the-free-apps-online-poker-affiliation-is-changing-once-again)). Agents attract players, process money, provide support and pay rakeback ([WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/blog/pppoker-agent-all-you-need-to-know)), and signing up through one "places the player in that agent's downline" ([PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/poker-sites/pokerbros-app-review)) — they are the only actors with trusted recurring contact with players and an economic interest in player longevity. PA Tools already distributes to this layer, letting players "check their own stats without messages or screenshots" ([PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/blog/pa-tools-overview-platform-for-poker-apps-agents-and-players)). A revenue-share or white-label offer to agents and affiliates is the natural B2B2C channel, and a rake-reconstruction feature that gives a player leverage in rakeback negotiations is both a hook for players and a reason agents might resist — a tension that needs testing. The trade-off is association with unlicensed money flows, which matters for Israeli banking and store optics. No case study of a poker tool growing through clubs or agents exists, and the assumption that these communities live on Telegram rests only on affiliates listing Telegram and WhatsApp as their own contact channels.

Pricing is bimodal and the gap between the modes is where this product lands. Serious study tools charge $39–$289 a month to a small base of grinders — GTO Wizard runs on roughly 47 staff in Prague with no disclosed funding or revenue ([Built In](https://builtin.com/company/gto-wizard)) and was named WSOP's 2026 training partner ([CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/de/2026/06/12/wsop-gto-wizard-poker-training-partner-2026/)) — while trackers charge $13–40 a month or $20–35 a year, and recreational reviewers anchor at "Well worth the $10" and churn at the first data-loss bug ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/316520188/poker-income-bankroll-tracker/reviews)). Three structural facts should shape the model. Willingness to pay attaches to perceived skill improvement rather than record-keeping: trainers charge three to four times tracker prices to the same user, and the one-time-purchase preference is the top review theme for trackers ([justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1073540690/poker-analytics-5-tracker/reviews)), so a free, reliable ledger with a paid review layer and a lifetime option around $200 (the market's answer to subscription fatigue, as with Poker Bankroll Tracker and Postflop+) matches observed behavior. The whole tracker category is likely under $1 million a year combined, so the business cannot be a $30-a-year ledger alone. And AI compute is being metered everywhere — Pokr sells "Energy" packs and GTO Wizard sells "Power Credits" ([Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6755294637); [PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/news/promotions/2026/10/gto-wizard-ultra-early-bird-offer-last-chance-52603.htm)) — which, given the sub-cent per-screenshot costs discussed below, is a margin opportunity rather than a cost problem. The reasonable landing zone is a free ledger, a Pro tier around Pokerbase's $12.99 a month justified by hand review and the tilt profile, and a ceiling well under GTO Wizard Starter's $39–49.

The club-adjacent business model also has a legitimacy dimension that doubles as a moat. Training sites monetize through annual commitments reviewers describe as "the version of this product that punishes stopping," where "subscriptions often go unused" ([Poker Skill](https://www.pokerskill.com/blog/poker-training-sites/)). A product whose value is delivered per session — a plan before, a review after — is positioned against that structure, and a product that operates only on player-owned, post-session data avoids the dependency on site permission that just cost PT4 and HM3 their largest market when GGPoker banned them ([PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2026/10/ggpoker-bans-huds-52622.htm)).

## Capture hands from the replay screen after the session, never from the live table

No club app writes a tracker-readable hand history, and the native hooks that do exist are few and specific. On ClubGG there is "not a standard export"; "some clubs let players email hand histories, but many don't," the Windows client "doesn't save hand histories to a local folder," ClubGG hands do not use GGPoker's format and PokerCraft does not parse them ([Deepfold](https://deepfold.co/en/blog/clubgg-hand-analysis)). PokerBros lets players email the last six days of hands as an XLS-style file with "columns and suit icons" that trackers cannot read ([KingsHands](https://kingshands.com/blog/gde-xranitsya-handhistory-prilozheniya-pokerbros/); [Professional Rakeback](https://professionalrakeback.com/poker-bros-app-review)) — structured, but short-lived and needing a custom parser. X-Poker has "no .txt export, no log files, no email request, and no hand history menu," but a finished hand can be shared as a web "Share Replay" link, and Deepfold's parser "reconstructs the hand history from the replay link" ([Deepfold](https://deepfold.co/en/blog/hand-converter-guide)) — the highest-fidelity, lowest-cost input anywhere in the ecosystem, and the single highest-value follow-up is checking whether ClubGG, PPPoker or Suprema have any equivalent shareable URL. PPPoker, per GTO Wizard's own help page, "requires paid converter that writes hands to disk" ([GTO Wizard help](https://help.gtowizard.com/where-to-get-your-hand-histories/)). The converter route — Windows, emulator, $29–30 a month, undisclosed capture method, broken by every client update, detectable by PokerBros — is the highest-risk path and the wrong fit for a mobile product; it should be excluded, not deprioritized.

That leaves the player's own screenshots of the post-session hand-history or replay screen, and the operating-system mechanics for making that nearly effortless are already in place. On iOS nothing can trigger a Shortcut when a screenshot is taken ([MPU forum](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/shortcut-triggered-from-screenshot-or-saved-video/36172)), but two flows cost about one tap per hand: a Back Tap or Action Button automation that takes the screenshot and posts it, or the normal screenshot followed by a Share Sheet "Action Extension" shortcut that receives images as input and can be restricted to image content ([Apple Shortcuts User Guide](https://support.apple.com/en-kg/guide/shortcuts/apd163eb9f95/2.1/ios)). On Android, MediaProjection requires fresh consent per session, a foreground service from Android 14, and shows a prominent status chip from Android 15 ([Android developers](https://developer.android.com/media/grow/media-projection?hl=en)), while open-source tools like Screenshot Tile expose a floating capture button and Tasker/MacroDroid automation ([F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/com.github.cvzi.screenshottile)). A multi-image share-to-app flow (iOS Action Extension, Android ACTION_SEND) is the most robust batch path and needs no special permissions. Continuous screen recording during play is technically possible on Android but should be ruled out: it is indistinguishable from a scraping HUD from the app's perspective, captures opponents in real time, and is exactly what ClubGG's "through the interface" clause and PPPoker's real-time-assistance language target. Capturing only the replay screen after the session is defensible under every app's framing — unless Screen Guard covers it, which is the open question that decides the ClubGG roadmap.

Vision-language models are good enough to parse these screens cheaply, provided the pipeline validates them. No benchmark exists for poker screenshots specifically, but on a 1,477-frame screen-text benchmark GPT-4o reached 76% character accuracy versus 57% for RapidOCR and 49% for EasyOCR, with VLM failure modes of substituted or "corrected" words and content-safety refusals on benign input ([arXiv 2502.06445](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06445)); a May 2026 structured-extraction comparison scored Gemini 3.1 Pro and GPT-5.5 at 10/10 with failures in formatting rather than misreads, and found a multi-image multi-turn flow failed where one image per call passed ([SimpleMetrics](https://simplemetrics.xyz/comparing-ai-vision-which-model-wins-in-image-recognition-and-text-extraction)). The one poker-specific anecdote found "suit confusion, such as identifying a King of Diamonds as a King of Hearts" and refused to "completely trust the output yet" ([Jamesin Seidel](https://jamesin.substack.com/p/i-built-an-ai-system-to-analyze-poker)). Cost is not the constraint: Claude Haiku 5.5 is priced at $0.10 per million input tokens with 50% off in batch ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)) and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite at $0.30 ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)), so a phone screenshot of one to two thousand image tokens costs a fraction of a cent on cheap tiers and a few cents on frontier tiers — well under a cent per session for 30 screenshots. The architecture the evidence supports is a hybrid: detect app and layout version by a corner or logo template; classify the 52 card glyphs by template matching or a small CNN, the approach every open-source scraper uses, with the caveat that dickreuter's matcher breaks at a 1% graphic change ([DeepWiki](https://deepwiki.com/dickreuter/Poker/2.2-screen-operations-and-image-recognition)); read stack and bet digits with a VLM under strict JSON schema output; then validate deterministically — per-street contributions must sum to the displayed pot, final stacks must equal starting stacks plus or minus net, no duplicate cards across hole cards and board, exactly 3/1/1 board cards per street — and re-ask with a cropped region or a second model on cards only when a check fails. Sending each screenshot in its own call and merging deterministically avoids the documented multi-turn failure.

Batch workflows must be built, because nobody has shipped one. Deepfold takes one hand at a time and caps at 30 a month ([Deepfold](https://deepfold.co/en/blog/hand-converter-guide)); Pokr's photo "Scan Showdown" is "coming soon" ([Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6755294637)). Splitting a dump of 30 screenshots into hands relies on screenshot timestamps (monotonic within a review session, reversed if the player scrolls newest-to-oldest), any hand ID or timestamp visible in the replay header once parsed, perceptual hashing for exact duplicates, and a fingerprint of hero cards, blinds, seat count and table name to merge multi-street views of one hand and detect the next. For storage, screenshot-derived hands are partial — no timestamps, often unknown villain cards, approximate stacks — so a JSON schema with explicit nullability is the right canonical store, with PokerStars text generated on export because it is what GTO Wizard, PT4 and HM3 consume ([GTO Wizard help](https://help.gtowizard.com/where-to-get-your-hand-histories/)). The Open Hand History JSON format exists but has almost no tooling momentum (two small repositories last touched 2023–2024) ([GitHub topic](https://github.com/topics/open-hand-history)), while the TOML-based PHH format has an active parser in PokerKit that could double as a stack-and-pot validator ([arXiv 2312.11753](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.11753v3)). A GTO Wizard feature request for CSV/JSON export so users can run hands through LLMs ([GTO Wizard Nolt](https://gto-wizard.nolt.io/712)) confirms demand for machine-readable hands on the review side.

## Purpose, ranked gaps, risks and what still needs real users

The product's purpose, stated plainly: give app-club poker players the one thing every other actor in their ecosystem has and they do not — a trustworthy, player-owned record of their own money, decisions and state of mind — and use that record to enforce, with friction rather than force, the commitments they make to themselves while calm, and to turn each session into a small, outcome-independent lesson. It is a ledger first, a discipline device second, and a coach third, in that order because the evidence says that is the order in which value is trusted, retained and paid for.

The gaps rank as follows. First, a club-native ledger: multi-currency with per-club chip rates, agent deposits and withdrawals as first-class ledger events, rebuys within a session, rake and rakeback reconstruction that gives the player a weekly rake statement without asking the agent, and records that double as dispute evidence — no tracker has any of this, and reliability (sync, backup, import) is the entire basis of trust in the category. Second, friction-enforced precommitment set at session start: self-authored stop-loss and time cap with low anchoring, an 80%-of-own-limit alert, a one-sec-style wait-and-exit on rebuys and deposits, delayed loosening and instant tightening, and off-plan deposits logged rather than hidden; the only shipping attempts have ~100 installs and no enforcement. Third, process-metric review that replaces the win-rate chart: flagged hands with reason tags, outcome-hidden grading as mistake/cooler/unclear, technical-versus-mental tallies trended over two-to-three-week windows, late-session and post-big-loss clustering as a tilt profile, preflop stability by position, bankroll health from a conservative win-rate bound, and bb/100 shown only inside a confidence band with a Results-Day cadence. Fourth, a batch screenshot pipeline for club apps with validation and no monthly hand cap, where the one competitor caps at 30 hands a month and the one screenshot-capable coach has three ratings. Fifth, agent and club distribution with a white-label or revenue-share offer, which no poker tool has tried. Israel is a wedge market for the first three — ILS tables, a founder inside the community, no competing Hebrew-language tooling — not the end market.

The key risks, in order of how much they could change the plan. ClubGG's Screen Guard: if it blacks out the replay screen by default, the ClubGG screenshot path dies on iOS and the roadmap must lead with the ledger and discipline layers, with hand capture via club-emailed histories or manual quick-log; this must be checked on a real device this week, and again after each ClubGG release. Club-level hostility: a tool that surfaces a player's rake can be framed by agents as a threat to their margin, and PPPoker explicitly leaves tool tolerance to club administrators, so the agent channel could just as easily become an agent blacklist. Platform policy drift: NSUS can attach a GGPoker-style policy to ClubGG through the "posted guidelines" clause without changing the June 2026 terms, and the 1 December 2026 GGPoker enforcement start is the date to watch. Logging adherence: all precommitment evidence comes from operators who enforce limits at the cashier and all logging evidence shows manual entry roughly halving by week ten, and nothing proves a recreational club player will log sessions, let alone grade hands; the Danish trials are a standing warning that lab-plausible nudges can do nothing or backfire in the field. Legal and banking optics in Israel: Procedure 411 merchant screening and any marketing that points to specific clubs or agents. Privacy: any drift toward a cross-user opponent database recreates the pattern PokerStars litigated against, and Amendment 13's thresholds for a small startup are unconfirmed. Finally, the unit economics of the ledger alone are too thin — the whole tracker category plausibly earns under $1 million a year — so the paid review layer has to convert.

The open questions that only real users can answer: Does ClubGG's Screen Guard, on iOS, Android and Windows, cover the hand-history and replay screen, and is it on by default? Do ClubGG, PPPoker and Suprema replays offer any shareable link or image, and how many screenshots does one hand actually take in each app? Will recreational club players log a session in three taps, and for how many weeks — is the week-ten decay curve from dietary logging the right prior, and does a weekly streak with two forgiveness tokens bend it? Does a wait-and-exit interrupt on "deposit" change reload behavior when the deposit itself happens in a chat with an agent outside the app, or does it just get skipped? Does a falling self-labeled mistake rate predict anything about later results, and does showing a player their own tilt profile change late-session play? Will agents distribute a tool that shows players their rake, or fight it? Does rake-and-rakeback reconstruction actually get players better deals, and does that become the word-of-mouth hook? What will an Israeli club player pay — the $10 one-off recreationals anchor on, Pokerbase's $12.99 a month, or something closer to GTO Wizard's Starter — and does a lifetime price convert the subscription-averse? And do the vision-model accuracy and validation rates on real ClubGG, PokerBros and X-Poker replay screens — especially suits and stack digits — reach the level where players trust the parsed hand without re-checking it, since trust in the ledger is the one thing the category never forgives losing.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the product from "a poker app with AI hand review" to "the missing institution of club poker." Regulated-site players have a cashier, a published rake schedule, PokerCraft, exportable histories and a support desk; club players have an agent and a chat window. Every gap this report ranks is a consequence of that missing institution, and the behavioral evidence happens to point in the same direction as the market gap: the mechanics that work — self-authored limits set before play, friction at the moment of action, personal-threshold alerts, forgiving habits — are all things a player-owned ledger can do and an operator with a conflict of interest will not. That alignment is the strategic case.

It also changes how the roadmap should be sequenced and judged. The ledger and the discipline layer do not depend on Screen Guard, on any app's goodwill, or on vision-model accuracy; they depend on a three-tap log that survives week ten, which is the cheapest hypothesis to test and the one everything else rests on. Hand capture and AI review are where the eventual willingness to pay lives, but they inherit every platform risk in this report, so they should be built to degrade gracefully — from replay links to screenshots to a quick-log keyboard — rather than assumed. And because the central learning claim (that outcome-independent process metrics predict later results) has never been tested anywhere, the app that ships it will be producing the evidence, not consuming it; instrumenting for that from day one is both the research contribution and the defensible moat.
